# 150 Essential Phrasal Verbs

## What is a phrasal verb?
A phrasal verb = **verb + small word** (up, down, in, out, on, off, away, back...). Together they have a **new meaning**.
- "**give**" = hand something to someone. "**give up**" = stop trying.
- "**look**" = use your eyes. "**look after**" = take care of.
Native speakers use phrasal verbs **all the time**, especially in conversation. Learning them makes you sound natural.
### Separable or not?
Some phrasal verbs can be separated by the object:
- **Turn off** the light. = **Turn** the light **off**. ✓
- With a pronoun, you **must** separate: **Turn it off**. ✓ (not "Turn off it")
Others cannot be separated: "**Look after** the baby." (not "Look the baby after")

## What are collocations?
Collocations are words that **naturally go together**. A native speaker says "**make** a mistake", not "**do** a mistake". There is no logical rule – you just need to learn them. Using correct collocations makes your English sound **natural**.

# Confusing Words

## Words that learners often mix up
| Words | Difference | Example |
| say / tell | say something; tell someone something | She **said** hello. She **told** me a story. |
| speak / talk | speak = more formal, languages; talk = conversation | I **speak** Hindi. We **talked** for hours. |
| see / look / watch | see = notice; look = direct your eyes; watch = look for a time at moving things | I **saw** a bird. **Look** at this! I **watch** TV. |
| hear / listen | hear = sounds come to you; listen = pay attention | I **heard** a noise. **Listen** to me! |
| borrow / lend | borrow = take; lend = give | Can I **borrow** your pen? Can you **lend** me your pen? |
| bring / take | bring = to here; take = to there | **Bring** your book tomorrow. **Take** this to your mother. |
| come / go | come = towards the speaker; go = away | **Come** here! **Go** to your room! |
| remember / remind | remember = not forget; remind = make someone remember | I **remember** her name. **Remind** me to call him. |
| learn / teach | learn = get knowledge; teach = give knowledge | I'm **learning** English. She **teaches** maths. |
| win / earn / gain | win a prize/game; earn money from work; gain weight/experience | We **won** the match. He **earns** a good salary. |
| lose / loose | lose = verb (not have); loose = adjective (not tight) | Don't **lose** your keys. These trousers are **loose**. |
| rob / steal | rob a person/place; steal a thing | They **robbed** a bank. They **stole** money. |
| fun / funny | fun = enjoyable; funny = makes you laugh | The party was **fun**. The joke was **funny**. |
| affect / effect | affect = verb; effect = noun | Smoking **affects** your health. It has a bad **effect**. |
| advice / advise | advice = noun; advise = verb | Good **advice**. I **advise** you to rest. |
| accept / except | accept = agree to take; except = not including | I **accept** your offer. Everyone came **except** Ali. |
| then / than | then = time; than = comparison | First eat, **then** sleep. She's taller **than** me. |
| quite / quiet | quite = fairly; quiet = not noisy | It's **quite** good. Please be **quiet**. |
| live / life | live = verb; life = noun | I **live** here. **Life** is beautiful. |
| sometime / sometimes | sometime = one day; sometimes = occasionally | Let's meet **sometime**. I **sometimes** walk. |
| every day / everyday | every day = each day; everyday = normal (adjective) | I run **every day**. **everyday** clothes |
| among / between | between = two; among = more than two | **between** you and me / **among** friends |
| ago / before | ago = from now; before = from a past time | two years **ago** / I had been there two years **before** |
| alone / lonely | alone = without others; lonely = sad because alone | I live **alone**, but I'm not **lonely**. |
| actually / currently | actually = in fact; currently = now | **Actually**, I'm 30. I'm **currently** working in Pune. |
| control / check | control = have power over; check = examine | **Check** your answers. (not "control your answers") |
| sensible / sensitive | sensible = wise; sensitive = easily hurt / feels a lot | a **sensible** decision / **sensitive** skin |
| embarrassed / pregnant | embarrassed = shy, ashamed (be careful – some languages have a false friend!) | I was **embarrassed** when I fell. |
| economic / economical | economic = about the economy; economical = saves money | **economic** growth / an **economical** car |
| historic / historical | historic = important in history; historical = about history | a **historic** day / a **historical** film |
| shade / shadow | shade = cool area out of the sun; shadow = dark shape | sit in the **shade** / my **shadow** on the wall |
| job / work | job = countable (a job); work = uncountable | I have **a job**. I have a lot of **work**. |
| travel / trip / journey | travel = general (uncountable/verb); trip = a short visit; journey = going from A to B | I love **travel**. a **trip** to Goa / a long **journey** |
| hard / hardly | hard = with effort; hardly = almost not | Work **hard**. I **hardly** slept. |
| late / lately | late = not on time; lately = recently | I was **late**. I've been busy **lately**. |
| as / like | as = in the role of; like = similar to | He works **as** a chef. He cooks **like** a chef. |
EX: Choose the correct word
1. Can you (say / tell) me your name? || tell
2. I (heard / listened) a strange noise. || heard
3. Please (bring / take) this letter to the post office. || take
4. I (lost / loosed) my wallet. || lost
5. She is taller (then / than) me. || than

7. The film was very (fun / funny). I laughed a lot. || funny

## Politeness mistakes
! Give me water. => **Could I have** some water, please?
! I want the bill. => **Could we have** the bill, please?
! What? (when you didn't hear) => **Sorry? / Pardon? / Could you say that again, please?**
! Tell me your name. => **May I have / Could I have** your name, please?
! You are wrong. => **I'm not sure that's right.** / **I see it differently.**
! Come here. (to a stranger) => **Excuse me, could you come here for a moment, please?**
! I don't understand you. (rude tone) => **Sorry, I didn't quite understand. Could you explain?**
> **Tip:** In English, "please", "thank you", "sorry" and "excuse me" are used **much more** than in many other languages. Use them all the time! Questions ("Could you...?") are more polite than orders ("Do this.").
