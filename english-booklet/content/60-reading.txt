# Reading Practice: 10 Short Stories

## How to read in English
- **First read**: read quickly for the general idea. Don't stop for every new word.
- **Second read**: read slowly. Underline new words. Guess their meaning from the sentence.
- **Third read**: read **out loud**. This helps your pronunciation and fluency.
- Then answer the questions and check the answer key.
- Write 5 new words from each story in your notebook.
> **Tip:** Read something in English **every day** – even 10 minutes. Start with easy texts (children's stories, graded readers, simple news websites). Reading is the fastest way to build vocabulary.

## Story 1: The New Job (Level: Beginner)
Maya is 22 years old. She lives in a small town with her parents and her younger brother. Last month, she finished college. Yesterday, she got her first job! She is going to work in a bank in the city.
Maya is very happy, but she is also nervous. The city is big and busy. She has to take a bus every morning at 7 o'clock. The journey takes one hour.
On her first day, Maya wore a new blue dress. Her mother made her a big breakfast. "Don't worry," said her father. "Everyone is nervous on the first day."
At the bank, her manager was very kind. He showed her the office and introduced her to her colleagues. At lunchtime, a woman called Rita invited Maya to eat with her. They talked and laughed. In the evening, Maya called her mother. "It was a great day," she said. "I think I'm going to love this job."
EX: Story 1 questions
1. How old is Maya? || She is 22 (years old).
2. Where is her new job? || In a bank in the city.
3. How long does the bus journey take? || One hour.
4. What did Maya wear on her first day? || A new blue dress.
5. Who invited Maya to lunch? || Rita (a colleague).
6. How did Maya feel at the end of the day? || Happy – she said it was a great day.

## Story 2: The Lost Wallet (Level: Beginner)
One rainy evening, Sam was walking home from work. Near the bus stop, he saw something on the ground. It was a brown leather wallet. He picked it up and opened it. Inside, there was a lot of money – about 10,000 rupees – and an ID card. The name on the card was "Mr Harish Gupta", and there was an address.
Sam thought for a moment. He didn't have much money himself. But he knew it was wrong to keep it. The address was not far, so he walked there.
An old man opened the door. "Excuse me, are you Mr Gupta?" Sam asked. "Yes, I am," said the man. "I think this is yours," Sam said, and he gave him the wallet.
The old man's eyes filled with tears. "Thank you! This money was for my wife's medicine. I looked everywhere for it!" He tried to give Sam some money, but Sam said no. "Then please, come in and have some tea," said Mr Gupta. That evening, Sam made a new friend.
EX: Story 2 questions
1. What was the weather like? || It was rainy.
2. What did Sam find? || A (brown leather) wallet.
3. How much money was in it? || About 10,000 rupees.
4. Why was the money important to Mr Gupta? || It was for his wife's medicine.
5. Did Sam take money from Mr Gupta? || No, he didn't.
6. Find a word in the story that means "drops of water from your eyes". || tears

## Story 3: A Day at the Beach (Level: Elementary)
Last summer, the Fernandes family went to Goa for a week. On their third day, they decided to spend the whole day at the beach.
They woke up early and packed a big bag with towels, sunscreen, sandwiches, fruit and lots of water. When they arrived at the beach, it was still quiet. The sea was calm and blue, and the sand was warm.
The children, Leo and Mia, ran straight into the water. Their father, Antony, sat under a big umbrella and read a book, while their mother, Clara, went for a long walk along the beach. She collected some beautiful shells.
At lunchtime, they ate their sandwiches and fruit. In the afternoon, the beach became very crowded. Leo and Mia built a huge sandcastle, and some other children helped them. Later, the whole family went on a boat trip and they saw dolphins jumping out of the water!
In the evening, they watched the sun go down over the sea. "This is the best day of the holiday," said Mia. Everyone agreed.
EX: Story 3 questions
1. How long did the family stay in Goa? || A week.
2. What did they pack in their bag? || Towels, sunscreen, sandwiches, fruit and water.
3. What did Clara collect? || Shells.
4. What did the children build? || A (huge) sandcastle.
5. What animals did they see on the boat trip? || Dolphins.
6. Find the opposite of "quiet" in paragraph 4. || crowded

## Story 4: The Clever Crow (Level: Elementary – a traditional fable)
On a very hot summer day, a crow was flying in the sky. He was extremely thirsty. He flew here and there looking for water, but he couldn't find any. The rivers and ponds were dry.
At last, he saw a pot in a garden. He flew down quickly and looked inside. There was some water at the bottom, but the pot was tall, and his beak couldn't reach it.
The crow tried to push the pot over, but it was too heavy. He felt tired and hopeless. Then he had an idea. He saw some small stones near the pot. He picked up a stone with his beak and dropped it into the pot. Then another one, and another one.
Slowly, the water began to rise. The crow continued for a long time. Finally, the water was high enough. The crow drank the cool water happily and then flew away.
**Moral**: Where there's a will, there's a way. (If you really want something, you can find a way to get it.)
EX: Story 4 questions
1. Why was the crow looking for water? || Because he was (extremely) thirsty.
2. Why couldn't he drink the water at first? || The pot was tall and his beak couldn't reach the water.
3. What did he put in the pot? || Small stones.
4. What happened to the water? || It rose (came up).
5. What is the moral of the story? || Where there's a will, there's a way.

## Story 5: Learning to Swim at 40 (Level: Pre-intermediate)
For most of his life, Vikram was afraid of water. When he was seven, he fell into a river near his village. His uncle pulled him out, but Vikram never forgot the fear. Since that day, he had never gone into deep water.
Last year, Vikram's daughter, Aisha, started swimming lessons. Every Saturday, Vikram sat beside the pool and watched her. "Papa, why don't you swim with me?" she asked one day. Vikram felt embarrassed. "I can't swim," he admitted.
That night, he couldn't sleep. He was 40 years old. Was it too late? The next morning, he spoke to Aisha's teacher. "I've never been able to swim," he said, "but I'd like to try." The teacher smiled. "It's never too late. I've taught people who were 70!"
The first lessons were very difficult. Vikram's heart was beating fast every time he put his face in the water. But the teacher was patient, and Aisha encouraged him. Slowly, he became more confident. After three months, he swam a whole length of the pool for the first time. Aisha shouted and clapped.
Now, Vikram and Aisha swim together every weekend. "I wasted 33 years being afraid," he says. "Don't let fear stop you from learning something new – like English!"
EX: Story 5 questions
1. Why was Vikram afraid of water? || Because he fell into a river when he was seven.
2. Who started swimming lessons first? || His daughter, Aisha.
3. What did the teacher say about age? || It's never too late. She/he had taught people who were 70.
4. How did Vikram feel during the first lessons? || Nervous/afraid – his heart was beating fast.
5. How long did it take him to swim a whole length? || Three months.
6. What is the main message of the story? || Don't let fear stop you from learning something new.

## Story 6: The Power of Habit (Level: Pre-intermediate)
Ananya wanted to read more books. Every January, she made the same promise: "This year, I'm going to read 30 books." And every year, she read only two or three.
One day, she watched a video about habits. The speaker said: "Don't try to make big changes. Make your new habit so small that it's impossible to fail." Ananya decided to try. Her new rule was very simple: read **just two pages every night** before sleeping.
Two pages took her only three minutes. It was so easy that she never missed a day. But something interesting happened. Many nights, after two pages, she wanted to continue. Often she read twenty or thirty pages.
By December, Ananya had read 24 books – ten times more than before! "The secret," she says, "isn't motivation. It's starting small and doing it every single day."
You can use the same idea for English. Don't promise to study three hours a day. Promise to learn **five words** a day, or to speak English for **five minutes**. Small steps, every day, become big results.
EX: Story 6 questions
1. How many books did Ananya usually read in a year? || Two or three.
2. What was her new rule? || Read just two pages every night before sleeping.
3. How long did two pages take? || About three minutes.
4. How many books did she read by December? || 24.
5. According to Ananya, what is the secret? || Starting small and doing it every single day (not motivation).

## Story 7: The Interview (Level: Intermediate)
Karan had applied for more than twenty jobs since graduating, but he had never been invited to an interview. When the email from a large software company finally arrived, he read it three times to make sure it was real.
The interview was going to be in English. Karan's technical skills were strong, but his spoken English was weak. He had always been too shy to practise. He had exactly two weeks to prepare.
He made a plan. Every morning, he wrote down five common interview questions and prepared answers. Every evening, he recorded himself answering them on his phone. When he listened back, he cringed – he spoke too fast, he said "um" constantly, and he made the same grammar mistakes again and again. But he kept recording. He also asked his cousin, who worked in Canada, to do a practice interview with him on a video call.
By the day of the interview, Karan wasn't perfect, but he was ready. When the interviewer asked, "Tell me about yourself," he smiled, took a breath and spoke clearly. He didn't understand one question, so he said, "I'm sorry, could you rephrase that, please?" – a phrase he had practised many times.
A week later, he received the offer. "They told me my English was clear and confident," he said. "Two weeks of daily practice changed my life."
EX: Story 7 questions
1. How many jobs had Karan applied for? || More than twenty.
2. What was Karan's main problem? || His spoken English was weak / he was too shy to practise.
3. What did he do every evening? || He recorded himself answering interview questions.
4. Who helped him with a practice interview? || His cousin (who worked in Canada).
5. What did Karan say when he didn't understand a question? || "I'm sorry, could you rephrase that, please?"
6. What does "cringed" probably mean? || felt embarrassed (made an uncomfortable face)

## Story 8: Two Neighbours (Level: Intermediate)
Mrs Kaur and Mr Ahmed had lived next door to each other for fifteen years, but they had hardly ever spoken. Years ago, they'd had an argument about a tree. Its branches grew over Mr Ahmed's wall, and leaves fell into his garden. Since then, they had only nodded coldly when they passed in the street.
One winter night, there was a terrible storm. The electricity went off in the whole street. At about midnight, Mr Ahmed heard someone knocking. It was Mrs Kaur, holding a candle. She was shaking. "I'm sorry to disturb you," she said, "but water is coming into my kitchen, and I don't know what to do."
Mr Ahmed didn't hesitate. He took his torch and some tools and followed her. A pipe had broken. He turned off the water and fixed the pipe as well as he could. It took nearly two hours. By the end, they were both wet and exhausted – and laughing.
"I've been silly about that tree," Mrs Kaur said, making tea on her gas stove. "No," said Mr Ahmed, "I was the silly one." From that night on, they became good friends. Every summer, Mr Ahmed now helps Mrs Kaur pick the fruit from the tree – and she always gives him half.
EX: Story 8 questions
1. How long had they been neighbours? || Fifteen years.
2. Why did they stop speaking? || Because of an argument about a tree.
3. Why did Mrs Kaur knock on Mr Ahmed's door? || Water was coming into her kitchen (a pipe had broken).
4. How long did the repair take? || Nearly two hours.
5. What happens every summer now? || Mr Ahmed helps her pick the fruit and she gives him half.
6. Find a word that means "stop for a moment because you are not sure". || hesitate

## Story 9: The Smartphone Experiment (Level: Intermediate)
Last month, a school in Bangalore did an interesting experiment. They asked 100 teenagers to give up their smartphones for one week. Some students laughed. Some were angry. "A whole week? Impossible!" said one boy.
The first two days were the hardest. Many students said they felt anxious and bored. They kept reaching for their pockets, looking for a phone that wasn't there. "I didn't know what to do with my hands," said Divya, 15.
But by the fourth day, things started to change. Students began talking more to each other during breaks. Some started reading books from the library. A group of boys organised a cricket match. Several students said they were sleeping better and feeling less tired in class.
At the end of the week, the school asked the students to write about their experience. Most of them said it had been difficult but useful. "I realised I was spending six hours a day on my phone," wrote one girl. "That's 42 hours a week! I could learn a new language with that time."
Not everyone changed their habits after the experiment. But about half of the students decided to reduce their screen time, and many said they would try the challenge again.
EX: Story 9 questions
1. How many students took part? || 100 teenagers.
2. How did students feel on the first two days? || Anxious and bored.
3. Name two things students started doing by the fourth day. || Talking more, reading books, playing cricket, sleeping better (any two).
4. How many hours a day was one girl spending on her phone? || Six hours.
5. Did all the students change their habits? || No – about half decided to reduce their screen time.

## Story 10: A Letter to My Younger Self (Level: Upper-intermediate)
*Dear 18-year-old me,*
*I know you're scared. You think your English is terrible, and you're embarrassed every time you open your mouth. You stay silent in class because you're afraid people will laugh. Let me tell you something: twenty years from now, you will give a presentation in English to 300 people – and you will enjoy it.*
*It won't happen by magic. It will happen because you'll make a decision to stop waiting until you feel "ready". You'll never feel completely ready. Nobody does. You'll start speaking anyway – badly at first, then a bit better, then quite well.*
*You'll make thousands of mistakes. Once, you'll tell a customer you're "very exciting" instead of "very excited" and everyone will laugh – including you. That's fine. Those mistakes are how you'll learn.*
*Here's my advice: listen to English every single day, even when you don't understand. Read things you enjoy. Talk to yourself in the shower. Write a few sentences each night about your day. Don't compare yourself with others – compare yourself with who you were last month.*
*Most importantly, be kind to yourself. Learning a language is one of the hardest and most rewarding things you'll ever do. I'm proud of you for starting.*
*With love,*
*Your future self*
EX: Story 10 questions
1. Why did the writer stay silent in class? || Because they were afraid people would laugh.
2. What will the writer do in twenty years? || Give a presentation in English to 300 people.
3. What mistake did the writer make with a customer? || Said "very exciting" instead of "very excited".
4. Who should you compare yourself with, according to the letter? || Yourself last month (who you were last month).
5. Give two pieces of advice from the letter. || Listen every day; read things you enjoy; talk to yourself; write sentences each night; be kind to yourself (any two).

# Listening Skills

## Why is listening difficult?
Many learners say: "I can read English, but I can't understand when people speak!" This is very normal. Here's why:
- Native speakers speak **fast** and **join words together** ("Whaddaya want?" = "What do you want?").
- They use **weak forms** – small words almost disappear.
- There are many different **accents**: British, American, Australian, Indian, Nigerian and more.
- Real conversations have **idioms, slang and phrasal verbs**.
The good news: your ears can be **trained**, like a muscle. The more you listen, the better you understand.

## A step-by-step listening method
1. **Choose the right level.** If you understand less than 50%, it's too difficult. If you understand 100%, it's too easy. Aim for **70–80%**.
2. **Listen once** for the general idea. Don't stop.
3. **Listen again** with subtitles **in English** (not your language).
4. **Listen a third time** without subtitles. You'll be surprised how much more you understand.
5. **Shadow** (see below): repeat what the speaker says, at the same time.
6. **Write down** 3–5 useful phrases you heard.

## Shadowing: the secret technique
Shadowing means listening to a speaker and **repeating immediately** after them, like a shadow, copying their speed, rhythm and intonation.
- Choose a short audio (30–60 seconds) with a transcript.
- Listen and read the transcript.
- Play again, and speak **along with** the speaker, one or two words behind.
- Repeat 5–10 times until you can keep up.
This trains your **ears and mouth together**. It is one of the best ways to improve pronunciation and fluency.

## What to listen to
| Level | Suggestions |
| Beginner | Children's cartoons, simple songs, "slow English" podcasts and YouTube channels, graded audiobooks |
| Elementary | YouTube channels for English learners, simple podcasts, short news for learners, cooking videos |
| Intermediate | TV series (with English subtitles), TED talks, podcasts about your hobbies, YouTube vlogs, audiobooks |
| Advanced | Films without subtitles, news radio, interviews, debates, comedy shows |
> **Tip:** Listen to topics you **love**. If you like cricket, listen to cricket commentary. If you like cooking, watch cooking videos in English. When you are interested, you learn faster and you don't get bored.

# Writing: Sentences and Paragraphs

## Writing good sentences
A good sentence:
- starts with a **capital letter** and ends with a **full stop**, question mark or exclamation mark;
- has a **subject** and a **verb**;
- expresses **one clear idea**.
### Make simple sentences longer and better
| Simple | Better |
| I have a dog. | I have a small brown dog called Max. |
| I went to the market. | Yesterday, I went to the market to buy vegetables. |
| The film was good. | The film was really exciting, and the actors were excellent. |
| I like my job. | I like my job because my colleagues are friendly and I learn new things every day. |
### Avoid very long sentences
! I woke up and I had breakfast and I went to work and I had a meeting and then I had lunch and it was good. => I woke up, had breakfast and went to work. After a meeting, I had a delicious lunch.

## Writing a paragraph
A paragraph is a group of sentences about **one main idea**. It has three parts:
1. **Topic sentence** – tells the main idea.
2. **Supporting sentences** – give details, reasons and examples.
3. **Concluding sentence** – finishes the idea.
### Example paragraph: My Favourite Place
"**My favourite place is my grandmother's village in Kerala.** It is very green and peaceful, with coconut trees and small rivers everywhere. When I visit, I wake up to the sound of birds instead of traffic. My grandmother cooks delicious fish curry, and in the evenings we sit on the veranda and talk for hours. I also love swimming in the river with my cousins. **For me, this village is the perfect place to relax and forget about the stress of city life.**"
### Example paragraph: The Benefits of Exercise
"**Regular exercise has many benefits for both the body and the mind.** First, it makes your heart and muscles stronger and helps you control your weight. Second, it gives you more energy during the day and helps you sleep better at night. In addition, exercise reduces stress. For example, a 30-minute walk can make you feel calmer and happier. **In short, everyone should try to exercise for at least a few minutes every day.**"

## Your daily diary
Writing a short diary every day is a wonderful habit. Use this model:
"**Today** I woke up at 7. **In the morning**, I went to work by bus. **At work**, I had a long meeting. It was quite boring. **For lunch**, I ate rice and dal with my colleagues. **In the afternoon**, I finished a report. **After work**, I went to the gym. **In the evening**, I watched a film and studied English for 30 minutes. **Today I learned** the phrase "call it a day". **Tomorrow I'm going to** visit my aunt."
EX: Improve the sentences
1. i like cricket || I like cricket. (Better: I really like cricket because it's exciting to watch with friends.)
2. She is teacher. || She is a teacher.
3. Yesterday I go to the park. || Yesterday I went to the park.
4. The food was good. (make it more interesting) || Example: The food was delicious, especially the spicy chicken curry.

# Writing Emails and Messages

## Informal vs formal
| | Informal (friends, family) | Formal (work, officials, strangers) |
| Greeting | Hi Sam, / Hey Sam! / Dear Sam, | Dear Mr Patel, / Dear Ms Rao, / Dear Sir or Madam, |
| Opening | Hope you're well! / How are things? / Thanks for your message. | I hope this email finds you well. / I am writing to... / Thank you for your email. |
| Asking | Can you...? / Could you...? | Could you please...? / I would be grateful if you could... |
| Closing line | Write soon! / Talk soon! / Can't wait to see you! | I look forward to hearing from you. / Please let me know if you have any questions. |
| Signing off | Love, / Take care, / Cheers, / Best, | Kind regards, / Best regards, / Yours sincerely, (if you know the name) / Yours faithfully, (if you started with Dear Sir or Madam) |
| Style | short forms (I'm, don't), simple words | full forms (I am, do not), polite words |

# Writing a CV and Other Documents

## A simple CV structure
| Section | What to include |
| Personal details | Full name, phone number, email, city (no need for age, religion or a photo in many countries) |
| Profile / summary | 2–3 sentences about who you are and what you can offer |
| Work experience | Most recent job first: job title, company, dates, 3–5 bullet points of achievements |
| Education | Degree, university, year; school if you are a recent graduate |
| Skills | Languages, computer skills, technical skills |
| Interests (optional) | 2–3 interests that show something positive |
| References | "Available on request" |
### Strong action verbs for your CV
managed, led, organised, created, developed, improved, increased, reduced, trained, designed, handled, solved, achieved, delivered, coordinated, supported, launched, planned
### Example bullet points
- **Managed** a team of five sales staff.
- **Increased** monthly sales by 20% in one year.
- **Handled** over 50 customer enquiries per day.
- **Trained** new employees in company software.
