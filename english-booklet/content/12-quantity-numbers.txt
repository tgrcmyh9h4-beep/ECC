# Some, Any, Much, Many and Other Quantifiers

## Some and any
| Word | Used in | Example |
| some | positive sentences | I have **some** friends in Delhi. There is **some** milk. |
| any | negatives | I don't have **any** money. There aren't **any** eggs. |
| any | questions | Do you have **any** questions? Is there **any** sugar? |
| some | offers and requests | Would you like **some** tea? Can I have **some** water? |
"Any" in a positive sentence means "it doesn't matter which": "Take **any** seat you like."

## Much, many, a lot of
| Word | With | Example |
| many | countable plural (mostly questions/negatives) | How **many** brothers do you have? There aren't **many** people. |
| much | uncountable (mostly questions/negatives) | How **much** money do you need? I don't have **much** time. |
| a lot of / lots of | both (mostly positive sentences) | She has **a lot of** books. We drank **lots of** water. |
! How much people? => How **many** people?
! I have much money. => I have **a lot of** money.
! There are much cars. => There are **many / a lot of** cars.

## A few, a little, few, little
| | Countable | Uncountable |
| small amount (positive) | **a few** friends | **a little** sugar |
| almost none (negative) | **few** friends | **little** hope |
- I have **a few** friends here. (some – I'm happy)
- I have **few** friends here. (almost none – I'm lonely)
- Can I have **a little** milk in my tea?
- There is **little** time left. Hurry!

## Too much, too many, enough
- There are **too many** cars in the city. (countable)
- I ate **too much** food. (uncountable)
- Do we have **enough** chairs for everyone?

## All, most, some, none, both, either, neither
- **All** my friends are students.
- **Most** people like music.
- **None of** us knew the answer.
- **Both** my parents are teachers.
- You can take **either** bus. (one or the other)
- **Neither** answer is correct. (not this one and not that one)
- **Each** student has a book. (every one, separately)
- **Every** day is a new chance.
! All of people => All people / All **the** people / All **of the** people
! Most of people => Most people

## How much / how many – asking about quantity
- **How many** children do you have?
- **How many** times have you been to Goa?
- **How much** is this shirt? (price)
- **How much** water do you drink every day?
EX: Choose the correct word
1. I don't have (some / any) brothers. || any
2. Would you like (some / any) coffee? || some
3. How (much / many) sugar do you want? || much
4. How (much / many) students are in your class? || many
5. There are (a lot of / much) people here. || a lot of
6. I have (a few / a little) money. Let's buy a snack. || a little
7. She has (a few / a little) good friends. || a few
8. There is (too much / too many) noise. || too much
9. Are there (some / any) shops near here? || any
10. (Both / Either) my sisters live in Canada. || Both

# Numbers, Dates, Time and Money

## Cardinal numbers
| | | | |
| 1 one | 11 eleven | 21 twenty-one | 100 a / one hundred |
| 2 two | 12 twelve | 30 thirty | 101 a hundred and one |
| 3 three | 13 thirteen | 40 forty | 250 two hundred and fifty |
| 4 four | 14 fourteen | 50 fifty | 1,000 a / one thousand |
| 5 five | 15 fifteen | 60 sixty | 10,000 ten thousand |
| 6 six | 16 sixteen | 70 seventy | 100,000 a hundred thousand (one lakh) |
| 7 seven | 17 seventeen | 80 eighty | 1,000,000 a / one million |
| 8 eight | 18 eighteen | 90 ninety | 10,000,000 ten million (one crore) |
| 9 nine | 19 nineteen | 99 ninety-nine | 1,000,000,000 a / one billion |
| 10 ten | 20 twenty | 0 zero / "oh" | |
> **Tip:** Be careful with stress: thir**TEEN** (13) vs **THIR**ty (30), fif**TEEN** (15) vs **FIF**ty (50).
! two hundreds people => two **hundred** people (no -s after a number)
But: **hundreds of** people, **thousands of** stars (when there is no exact number).
! forty => fourty (wrong spelling!) – correct spelling is **forty**.

## Ordinal numbers (order)
1st first, 2nd second, 3rd third, 4th fourth, 5th fifth, 6th sixth, 7th seventh, 8th eighth, 9th ninth, 10th tenth, 11th eleventh, 12th twelfth, 13th thirteenth, 20th twentieth, 21st twenty-first, 22nd twenty-second, 23rd twenty-third, 30th thirtieth, 100th hundredth
- She lives on the **third** floor.
- Today is my **twenty-first** birthday.

## Dates
| Write | Say (British) | Say (American) |
| 5 May / 5th May | the fifth of May | May fifth |
| 14/02/2026 (UK) | the fourteenth of February, twenty twenty-six | – |
| 02/14/2026 (US) | – | February fourteenth, twenty twenty-six |
Years: 1999 = nineteen ninety-nine, 2005 = two thousand and five, 2026 = twenty twenty-six
Decades: the 1980s = the nineteen eighties

## Telling the time
| Time | Way 1 | Way 2 (digital) |
| 7:00 | seven o'clock | seven |
| 7:05 | five past seven | seven oh five |
| 7:15 | (a) quarter past seven | seven fifteen |
| 7:30 | half past seven | seven thirty |
| 7:40 | twenty to eight | seven forty |
| 7:45 | (a) quarter to eight | seven forty-five |
| 12:00 | noon / midday / midnight | twelve |
- **a.m.** = in the morning (after midnight, before noon). **p.m.** = after noon.
- "What time is it?" / "What's the time?" – "It's half past six."
- "What time does the train leave?" – "At 8:20."
! It's 6 o'clock p.m. => It's 6 p.m. / It's 6 o'clock in the evening.

## Money
- £5.50 = five pounds fifty
- $12.99 = twelve dollars ninety-nine (cents)
- ₹450 = four hundred and fifty rupees
- €2.75 = two euros seventy-five
- "How much is it?" / "How much does it cost?" – "It's 300 rupees."
- "That's expensive!" / "That's a good price." / "Can you give me a discount?"

## Maths in English
| Symbol | Say |
| 2 + 3 = 5 | two plus three equals (is) five |
| 7 – 4 = 3 | seven minus four equals three |
| 3 × 4 = 12 | three times four / three multiplied by four is twelve |
| 10 ÷ 2 = 5 | ten divided by two is five |
| 50% | fifty percent |
| 0.5 | (zero) point five |
| ½ ¼ ¾ | a half, a quarter, three quarters |
| ⅓ | a third |

## Days, months and seasons
- **Days**: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday (weekend = Saturday and Sunday)
- **Months**: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December
- **Seasons**: spring, summer, autumn (UK) / fall (US), winter; also the monsoon / rainy season
- **Time words**: second, minute, hour, day, week, fortnight (two weeks), month, year, decade (10 years), century (100 years)
- the day before yesterday – yesterday – today – tomorrow – the day after tomorrow

1. 15 || fifteen
2. 50 || fifty
3. 342 || three hundred and forty-two
4. 3rd || third
5. 12th || twelfth
6. 7:45 || (a) quarter to eight / seven forty-five
7. 9:30 || half past nine / nine thirty
8. 2026 (year) || twenty twenty-six
9. 1,500 || one thousand five hundred / fifteen hundred
10. 21st || twenty-first
