# The Passive Voice

## Active and passive
In an **active** sentence, the subject **does** the action:
- "The cat **ate** the fish." (focus on the cat)
In a **passive** sentence, the subject **receives** the action:
- "The fish **was eaten** by the cat." (focus on the fish)
We use the passive when:
- the **action** is more important than who did it: "English **is spoken** all over the world."
- we **don't know** who did it: "My bike **was stolen**."
- it is **obvious** who did it: "He **was arrested**." (by the police, of course)
- in formal or scientific writing: "The results **were analysed**."

## How to make it: be + past participle (V3)
The verb "be" changes for the tense. The V3 stays the same.
| Tense | Active | Passive |
| Present simple | They **make** cars here. | Cars **are made** here. |
| Present continuous | They **are building** a bridge. | A bridge **is being built**. |
| Past simple | Someone **stole** my bag. | My bag **was stolen**. |
| Past continuous | They **were painting** the house. | The house **was being painted**. |
| Present perfect | They **have sold** the house. | The house **has been sold**. |
| Past perfect | They **had cancelled** the flight. | The flight **had been cancelled**. |
| Future (will) | They **will open** a new shop. | A new shop **will be opened**. |
| Going to | They **are going to repair** the road. | The road **is going to be repaired**. |
| Modal | You **must sign** the form. | The form **must be signed**. |
| Modal | They **can't fix** it. | It **can't be fixed**. |

## By + agent
Add "by" + the person/thing only when it is important:
- "Romeo and Juliet" **was written by** Shakespeare.
- This picture **was painted by** my grandfather.
- The house **was destroyed by** a fire.

## Common passive phrases in daily life
- I **was born** in 1995. (never "I born" or "I am born")
- It **is made of** wood / glass / gold.
- It **is made in** China.
- She **was invited** to the wedding.
- The shop **is closed** on Sundays.
- The meeting **has been cancelled**.
- Breakfast **is included**.
- English **is spoken** here.
- **Is** this seat **taken**?
- I **was told** to wait here.
- He **got injured** in an accident. ("get" + V3 is informal passive)
- We **got married** in 2020.
! I born in Delhi. => I **was born** in Delhi.
! The letter was wrote yesterday. => The letter was **written** yesterday.
! This house built in 1990. => This house **was built** in 1990.
! The window was broke. => The window was **broken**.
EX: Change to passive
1. They clean the room every day. || The room is cleaned every day.
2. Someone stole my phone. || My phone was stolen.
3. They are building a new hospital. || A new hospital is being built.
4. They have cancelled the match. || The match has been cancelled.
5. They will deliver the parcel tomorrow. || The parcel will be delivered tomorrow.
6. Shakespeare wrote Hamlet. || Hamlet was written by Shakespeare.
7. You must wear a helmet. || A helmet must be worn.
8. People speak Spanish in Mexico. || Spanish is spoken in Mexico.
EX: Complete with the correct passive form
1. I ___ (born) in Lucknow. || was born
2. This bag ___ (make) of leather. || is made
3. The thief ___ (catch) yesterday. || was caught
4. Your car ___ (repair) at the moment. || is being repaired
5. The results ___ (announce) next week. || will be announced

# Reported Speech

## Direct and reported speech
**Direct speech** repeats the exact words: She said, "**I am tired**."
**Reported (indirect) speech** reports the meaning: She said (that) **she was tired**.

## Tense changes (backshift)
When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told), the tense usually moves **one step back**.
| Direct speech | Reported speech |
| Present simple: "I **work** here." | Past simple: He said he **worked** there. |
| Present continuous: "I **am cooking**." | Past continuous: She said she **was cooking**. |
| Past simple: "I **saw** him." | Past perfect: He said he **had seen** him. |
| Present perfect: "I **have finished**." | Past perfect: She said she **had finished**. |
| will: "I **will** call you." | would: He said he **would** call me. |
| can: "I **can** swim." | could: She said she **could** swim. |
| may: "I **may** come." | might: He said he **might** come. |
| must: "I **must** go." | had to: She said she **had to** go. |
> **Tip:** If the information is still true now, you don't have to change the tense: "He said he **lives** in Delhi." (He still lives there.)

## Other changes
| Direct | Reported |
| now | then / at that time |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next week | the week after / the following week |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
Pronouns change too: "**I** love **you**." → He said **he** loved **me**.

## Say or tell?
- **say** + (that) + sentence: She **said** (that) she was hungry. ("said to me" is possible but less common)
- **tell** + **person** + (that) + sentence: She **told me** (that) she was hungry.
! She said me she was hungry. => She **told me** she was hungry. / She **said** she was hungry.
! He told that he was busy. => He **said** that he was busy. / He **told me** that he was busy.

## Reported questions
The word order becomes **normal** (subject + verb). No question mark. No do/does/did.
| Direct question | Reported question |
| "Where **do you live**?" | She asked me where **I lived**. |
| "What **is** your name?" | He asked me what **my name was**. |
| "**Are** you married?" | She asked **if / whether** I was married. |
| "**Did** you see the film?" | He asked **if** I **had seen** the film. |
| "**Can** you help me?" | She asked **if** I **could** help her. |
! He asked me where did I live. => He asked me where **I lived**.

## Reported orders and requests
Use **tell / ask + person + (not) to + verb**:
- "Sit down." → The teacher **told us to sit** down.
- "Don't be late." → My boss **told me not to be** late.
- "Please help me." → She **asked me to help** her.

## Other reporting verbs
| Verb | Example |
| explain | He **explained** that the train was late. |
| promise | She **promised to call** me. |
| offer | He **offered to help**. |
| refuse | They **refused to pay**. |
| advise | The doctor **advised me to rest**. |
| suggest | She **suggested going** to the cinema. |
| apologise | He **apologised for being** late. |
| agree | We **agreed to meet** at 6. |
| warn | I **warned him not to touch** it. |
| remind | **Remind me to buy** milk. |
EX: Change to reported speech
1. "I am happy," she said. || She said (that) she was happy.
2. "I live in Mumbai," he said. || He said (that) he lived in Mumbai.
3. "We will come tomorrow," they said. || They said (that) they would come the next day.
4. "I saw the film yesterday," she said. || She said (that) she had seen the film the day before.
5. "I can't swim," he said. || He said (that) he couldn't swim.
6. "Where do you work?" she asked me. || She asked me where I worked.
7. "Are you hungry?" he asked. || He asked if/whether I was hungry.
8. "Close the door," the teacher told me. || The teacher told me to close the door.
9. "Don't touch it," she said to the children. || She told the children not to touch it.
10. "I have lost my keys," he said. || He said (that) he had lost his keys.
EX: Say or tell?
1. She ___ me a secret. || told
2. He ___ that he was tired. || said
3. Can you ___ me the time? || tell
4. They ___ goodbye and left. || said
5. Please ___ him to call me. || tell

# Gerunds and Infinitives

## What are they?
- **Gerund** = verb + -ing used like a noun: **swimming**, **reading**, **eating**
- **Infinitive** = to + verb: **to swim**, **to read**, **to eat**
Some verbs are followed by a gerund, some by an infinitive, and some by both.

## Gerund (-ing) after these verbs
| Verb | Example |
| enjoy | I **enjoy reading**. |
| like / love / hate (also + to) | She **loves dancing**. |
| don't mind | I **don't mind waiting**. |
| finish | Have you **finished eating**? |
| stop | **Stop talking**! |
| avoid | Try to **avoid eating** junk food. |
| consider | We're **considering moving**. |
| suggest | He **suggested going** out. |
| practise | You should **practise speaking**. |
| keep (= continue) | She **kept calling** me. |
| give up | He **gave up smoking**. |
| can't stand | I **can't stand waiting**. |
| miss | I **miss seeing** you. |
| imagine | **Imagine living** on the moon! |
| admit / deny | He **admitted stealing** the money. |
| risk | Don't **risk losing** your job. |
| feel like | I **feel like eating** pizza. |
| look forward to | I'm **looking forward to seeing** you. |
! I enjoy to read. => I enjoy **reading**.
! I look forward to see you. => I look forward to **seeing** you.
! He suggested to go. => He suggested **going**.

## Gerund after prepositions
After a preposition (in, on, at, of, for, about, without, before, after...), always use **-ing**:
- I'm interested **in learning** Spanish.
- She's good **at cooking**.
- Thank you **for helping** me.
- He left **without saying** goodbye.
- **Before going** to bed, I brush my teeth.
- I'm thinking **about changing** my job.
- How **about going** for a walk?

## Gerund as a subject
- **Swimming** is good exercise.
- **Learning** English takes time.
- **Smoking** is bad for your health.

## Infinitive (to + verb) after these verbs
| Verb | Example |
| want | I **want to learn** English. |
| need | You **need to sleep**. |
| decide | We **decided to stay**. |
| hope | I **hope to see** you soon. |
| plan | They **plan to travel**. |
| promise | He **promised to help**. |
| agree | She **agreed to come**. |
| refuse | He **refused to answer**. |
| learn | I'm **learning to drive**. |
| try (make an effort) | **Try to relax**. |
| would like / would love | I'**d like to go**. |
| offer | She **offered to pay**. |
| afford | I **can't afford to buy** a car. |
| seem | He **seems to be** happy. |
| forget / remember (see below) | Don't **forget to call** me. |
| manage | I **managed to finish** on time. |
| expect | I **expect to be** there by 6. |
| choose | She **chose to stay**. |
| pretend | He **pretended to be** asleep. |
| tend | People **tend to forget**. |

## Infinitive of purpose (why?)
Use **to + verb** to say **why** you do something:
- I went to the shop **to buy** some bread.
- She's saving money **to buy** a car.
- I'm learning English **to get** a better job.
! I went to the shop for buy bread. => I went to the shop **to buy** bread.
! I came here for to study. => I came here **to study**.

## Adjective + infinitive
- It's **easy to learn**.
- It's **nice to meet** you.
- I'm **happy to help**.
- It's **difficult to understand**.
- It's **important to sleep** well.

## Verbs with a different meaning
| Verb | + -ing | + to |
| stop | stop the activity: I **stopped smoking**. (I don't smoke now) | stop in order to do something: I **stopped to smoke**. (I stopped walking so I could smoke) |
| remember | memory of the past: I **remember meeting** her. | not forget to do: **Remember to lock** the door. |
| forget | forget a past event: I'll never **forget seeing** the Taj Mahal. | forget a task: I **forgot to call** her. |
| try | experiment: **Try drinking** warm water. | make an effort: I **tried to open** it, but I couldn't. |
| regret | sorry about the past: I **regret telling** him. | sorry to give bad news: We **regret to inform** you... |

## Make and let + base verb (no "to")
- My mother **made me clean** my room. (forced)
- My father **let me drive** his car. (allowed)
- **Let's go**!
- Don't **make me laugh**!
! She made me to wait. => She made me **wait**.
! Let me to help you. => Let me **help** you.
EX: Gerund or infinitive?
1. I enjoy ___ (swim). || swimming
2. She wants ___ (become) a doctor. || to become
3. They decided ___ (sell) the house. || to sell
4. Do you mind ___ (open) the window? || opening
5. I'm looking forward to ___ (meet) you. || meeting
6. He promised ___ (call) me. || to call
7. Stop ___ (make) that noise! || making
8. I went to the bank ___ (get) some money. || to get
9. She is good at ___ (draw). || drawing
10. We can't afford ___ (buy) a new car. || to buy
11. Thank you for ___ (help) me. || helping
12. I need ___ (talk) to you. || to talk
13. He gave up ___ (smoke) last year. || smoking
14. My parents let me ___ (stay) out late. || stay
15. Remember ___ (turn off) the lights. || to turn off

# Relative Clauses: Who, Which, That, Where, Whose

## What is a relative clause?
A relative clause gives more information about a noun. It helps you join two sentences.
- I have a friend. **She** lives in Paris. → I have a friend **who** lives in Paris.
- This is the book. I bought **it** yesterday. → This is the book **that** I bought yesterday.

## Relative pronouns
| Pronoun | For | Example |
| who | people | The man **who** called you is my uncle. |
| which | things, animals | The phone **which** I bought is great. |
| that | people or things (informal) | The film **that** we saw was boring. |
| where | places | This is the house **where** I was born. |
| when | times | I remember the day **when** we met. |
| whose | possession | That's the girl **whose** father is a pilot. |
| whom | people as object (formal) | The person **whom** I met was kind. |
! The man which lives here. => The man **who** lives here.
! The book who I read. => The book **which/that** I read.
! This is the man who he helped me. => This is the man who helped me. (don't repeat the pronoun)
! The city where I live in. => The city where I live. / The city (which) I live in.

## Can I leave out who/which/that?
Yes, when it is the **object** of the relative clause (there is another subject after it):
- The film (**that**) **we** saw was great. ✓ (we = subject)
- The man (**who**) **I** met was friendly. ✓
No, when it is the **subject**:
- The man **who** lives next door is a doctor. (You can't remove "who".)

## Defining and non-defining
- **Defining** (no commas): necessary information. "Students **who study hard** pass exams." (only those students)
- **Non-defining** (with commas): extra information. "My brother, **who lives in Delhi**, is a lawyer." (I have only one brother; the information is extra.)
In non-defining clauses, you **cannot** use "that": "My car, **which** is red, is very fast." (not "that")
EX: Complete with who, which, where or whose
1. A doctor is someone ___ helps sick people. || who
2. This is the restaurant ___ we had our first date. || where
3. I lost the watch ___ my father gave me. || which/that
4. That's the woman ___ son plays for India. || whose
5. The people ___ live upstairs are very noisy. || who/that
6. A fridge is a machine ___ keeps food cold. || which/that
7. Do you know a shop ___ I can buy cheap shoes? || where
8. He's the boy ___ bike was stolen. || whose
EX: Join the sentences
1. I have a friend. He speaks five languages. || I have a friend who speaks five languages.
2. This is the café. I met my wife here. || This is the café where I met my wife.
3. She bought a dress. It was very expensive. || She bought a dress which/that was very expensive.
4. I know a man. His daughter is an actress. || I know a man whose daughter is an actress.

# Linking Words and Conjunctions

## Why linking words matter
Linking words connect your ideas. They make your speaking and writing **smooth** and **clear**. Without them, you sound like a robot: "I was tired. I went to bed. I didn't sleep." With them: "I was tired, **so** I went to bed, **but** I couldn't sleep."

## The basic linkers
| Purpose | Words | Example |
| Adding | and, also, too, as well, in addition, moreover, furthermore, besides | I like tea **and** coffee. She speaks French **too**. |
| Contrast | but, however, although, though, even though, while, whereas, on the other hand, despite, in spite of | It was raining, **but** we went out. **Although** he's rich, he isn't happy. |
| Reason | because, because of, since, as, due to | I stayed home **because** I was sick. **Because of** the rain, the match was cancelled. |
| Result | so, therefore, as a result, that's why | I was hungry, **so** I ate. He didn't study. **As a result**, he failed. |
| Purpose | to, in order to, so that | I study **to** get a good job. Speak slowly **so that** everyone understands. |
| Condition | if, unless, as long as, in case | Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. |
| Time / sequence | first, then, next, after that, finally, before, after, when, while, as soon as, until | **First**, wash the rice. **Then** add water. **Finally**, cook it for 15 minutes. |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as, like | I love fruit, **such as** mangoes and bananas. |
| Opinion | in my opinion, I think, personally, I believe | **In my opinion**, the film was too long. |
| Summary | in conclusion, to sum up, overall, in short | **In conclusion**, exercise is important. |
| Emphasis | in fact, actually, especially, indeed | I'm not tired. **In fact**, I feel great. |

## Because vs because of
- **because** + subject + verb: "The flight was late **because** **the weather was bad**."
- **because of** + noun: "The flight was late **because of** **the bad weather**."

## Although / even though vs despite / in spite of
- **although / even though** + subject + verb: "**Although** **it was cold**, we went swimming."
- **despite / in spite of** + noun or -ing: "**Despite** **the cold**, we went swimming." "**In spite of** **being** tired, she finished the work."
! Although it was raining, but we went out. => **Although** it was raining, we went out. (don't use "although" and "but" together)
! Despite of the rain. => **Despite** the rain. / **In spite of** the rain.
! Because I was tired, so I slept. => **Because** I was tired, I slept. / I was tired, **so** I slept.

## So and such
- **so** + adjective / adverb: "It was **so hot**." "She speaks **so quickly**."
- **such** + (a/an) + adjective + noun: "It was **such a hot** day." "They are **such nice** people."
- so/such ... **that** (result): "It was **so** cold **that** we stayed inside." "It was **such** a good film **that** I watched it twice."

## Both ... and, either ... or, neither ... nor, not only ... but also
- **Both** my brother **and** my sister are doctors.
- You can **either** call me **or** send a message.
- **Neither** the bus **nor** the train was on time.
- She is **not only** clever **but also** very kind.

## Punctuation with linkers
- **However**, **therefore**, **moreover** usually start a new sentence and have a comma after: "I wanted to go. **However**, I was too tired."
- **But**, **so**, **and** join two parts of one sentence: "I wanted to go, **but** I was too tired."
EX: Choose the best linking word
1. I was hungry, (so / but) I made a sandwich. || so
2. She is rich, (but / so) she isn't happy. || but
3. We stayed at home (because / because of) the storm. || because of
4. (Although / Despite) he was ill, he went to work. || Although
5. (Although / Despite) the rain, we played football. || Despite
6. It was (so / such) a beautiful day. || such
7. The tea is (so / such) hot that I can't drink it. || so
8. I study hard (to / for) pass my exams. || to
9. Take a jacket (in case / unless) it gets cold. || in case
10. I like many sports, (such as / so) football and tennis. || such as
11. (First / Finally), boil the water. Then add the pasta. || First
12. She can speak (both / either) English and Hindi. || both

# Punctuation and Capital Letters

## Capital letters
Use a capital letter for:
- The **first word** of a sentence: "**T**he sun is hot."
- The word **I**: "My friend and **I** went out."
- **Names** of people and places: **P**riya, **L**ondon, **M**ount **E**verest
- **Days and months**: **M**onday, **J**uly (but not seasons: summer, winter)
- **Languages and nationalities**: **E**nglish, **J**apanese, **I**ndian
- **Titles**: **M**r **S**harma, **D**r **K**han, **P**rofessor **R**ao
- Names of **books, films, companies**: *The Lion King*, **G**oogle

## Main punctuation marks
| Mark | Name | Use | Example |
| . | full stop (UK) / period (US) | end of a sentence | I am tired. |
| , | comma | pauses, lists, after linkers | I bought apples, bananas and grapes. |
| ? | question mark | end of a question | Where are you? |
| ! | exclamation mark | strong feeling | Wow! That's amazing! |
| ' | apostrophe | short forms, possession | don't, it's, Ravi's car |
| " " | quotation marks | direct speech | She said, "Hello." |
| : | colon | before a list or explanation | You need three things: a pen, paper and time. |
| ; | semicolon | joins two related sentences | It was late; we went home. |
| - | hyphen | joins words | a well-known actor, twenty-one |
| ( ) | brackets | extra information | My boss (who is very kind) helped me. |
## Common comma rules
- After introductory words: "**However,** I was late." "**Yesterday,** I went to the market."
- In lists: "red, blue, green and yellow"
- Before "but" and "so" joining two sentences: "I like tea, **but** she likes coffee."
- After an if-clause at the start: "If it rains, we will stay home."
- Around extra information: "My sister, **a nurse**, works at night."
## Apostrophe mistakes
! Apple's for sale. => Apple**s** for sale. (plural – no apostrophe)
! The dog wagged it's tail. => The dog wagged **its** tail.
! In the 1990's => In the **1990s**
EX: Add capital letters and punctuation
1. my name is ali and i live in dubai || My name is Ali and I live in Dubai.
2. where do you work || Where do you work?
3. i bought eggs milk bread and butter || I bought eggs, milk, bread and butter.
4. we met on monday in june || We met on Monday in June.
5. its my brothers car || It's my brother's car.
