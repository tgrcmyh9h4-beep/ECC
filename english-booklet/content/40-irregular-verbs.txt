# How to Learn Vocabulary

## Learning words the smart way
You do not need 50,000 words to be fluent. The **2,000 most common words** cover about **80–90%** of everyday conversation. 3,000 words is enough for most situations. This part of the book gives you those words, organised by topic.
### Five methods that work
- **Learn in context.** Always learn a word with an example sentence. "Borrow" → "Can I **borrow** your pen?"
- **Learn word partners (collocations).** "heavy rain" (not "strong rain"), "make a mistake" (not "do a mistake").
- **Use spaced repetition.** Review new words after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks and 1 month. Flashcard apps (like Anki) do this automatically.
- **Use the word.** Within 24 hours, use each new word in a sentence – spoken or written. Words you use become yours.
- **Group words by topic.** It's easier to remember "kitchen words" together than random words.
### How many words per day?
**10 new words per day** = 300 words per month = 3,600 words per year. That is enough to become fluent! Don't try to learn 100 words in one day – you will forget them.
> **Tip:** For each topic in this part, choose the words you don't know. Write them in your notebook with your **own** example sentence. Then say the sentence out loud three times.

# Irregular Verbs: The Complete List

## How to learn irregular verbs
Irregular verbs are some of the most common verbs in English. Learn them in groups with similar patterns. Say all three forms out loud: "**go – went – gone**".

## Confusing pairs
| Verb | Forms | Example |
| lie (be flat) | lie – lay – lain | I **lay** on the bed for an hour. |
| lie (not tell the truth) | lie – lied – lied | He **lied** to me. |
| lay (put down) | lay – laid – laid | She **laid** the baby on the bed. |
| rise (go up, no object) | rise – rose – risen | The sun **rose** at 6. |
| raise (lift something) | raise – raised – raised | **Raise** your hand. |
| fall (drop) | fall – fell – fallen | I **fell** off my bike. |
| feel (experience) | feel – felt – felt | I **felt** sick. |
| fill (make full) | fill – filled – filled | She **filled** the glass. |
