# The Secrets of Fluency

## What fluency really means
Fluency is **not** knowing every word or never making mistakes. Fluency means you can:
- speak **without stopping** a lot to think;
- **understand** most of what people say in everyday situations;
- **express your ideas** clearly, even with simple words;
- **keep a conversation going**, and recover when you don't know a word.
Many fluent speakers use only simple grammar and common words. What makes them fluent is **practice** and **confidence**.

## Secret 1: Think in English
Translating in your head is slow. Train your brain to think directly in English:
- **Name things** around you: "cup, window, laptop, plant, fan..."
- **Describe what you're doing**: "I'm making tea. Now I'm adding sugar. It smells good."
- **Plan your day in English**: "First I'll go to the bank, then I'll buy vegetables..."
- **Talk to yourself**: in the shower, while walking, while cooking.
- **Ask yourself questions**: "What will I eat tonight? Why am I tired?"
Start with 5 minutes a day. After a few weeks, English thoughts will come naturally.

## Secret 2: Speak every day – even alone
You learn to speak by speaking, just as you learn to swim by swimming.
- **Read aloud** for 10 minutes every day.
- **Record yourself** talking about a topic for 1–2 minutes. Listen back. Repeat.
- **Speak in front of a mirror** – watch your mouth and practise confidence.
- **Find a speaking partner**: a friend, a family member, an online language exchange, an English club.
- **Use voice messages** in English with friends who are also learning.
- **Use an AI chat assistant** to practise conversations – ask it to correct your mistakes.

## Secret 3: When you don't know a word, go around it
Don't stop! Use simple words to explain:
- "It's a kind of..." – "It's a kind of fruit, it's yellow and long." (banana)
- "It's something you use for..." – "It's something you use for cutting paper." (scissors)
- "It's like a... but..." – "It's like a bus, but on rails." (train)
- "The person who..." – "the person who fixes water pipes" (plumber)
- "What's the word...? The opposite of 'cheap'." (expensive)
- "I don't know the word in English, but..."
This skill is called **paraphrasing**, and it is used by all fluent speakers.

## Secret 4: Learn chunks, not single words
Fluent speakers have hundreds of ready-made phrases in their memory. They don't build every sentence from zero. Learn chunks like:
- "I was wondering if..."
- "To be honest, ..."
- "It depends on..."
- "The thing is, ..."
- "As far as I know, ..."
- "I'm not sure, but I think..."
- "What I mean is..."
- "It's up to you."
- "That makes sense."
- "I couldn't agree more."
- "Let me think about it."
- "I'm looking forward to it."
- "It's not a big deal."
- "I'd rather not."
- "Have you ever thought about...?"
- "No wonder!"
- "It's worth trying."
- "I'm used to it."
- "I didn't mean to..."
- "Sounds like a plan!"

## Secret 5: Don't fear mistakes – fear silence
The only way to make no mistakes is to say nothing. And if you say nothing, you never improve. Native speakers usually **don't care** about your mistakes. They care about **understanding you**. Most of them will be impressed that you speak a second language!
> **Tip:** When you feel nervous, remember: **communication is more important than perfection**. Breathe, smile, speak slowly, and keep going.

## Secret 6: Make English part of your life
- Change your **phone and apps** to English.
- Follow English-speaking people on **social media** about topics you like.
- Watch **one episode** of a series in English every day.
- Listen to English **podcasts** while travelling or exercising.
- Write your **shopping lists**, **to-do lists** and **diary** in English.
- Read **news** in simple English.
- Sing **English songs** – read the lyrics and sing along.
- Play **games** in English.

## Secret 7: Be consistent
Thirty minutes every day for one year is about **180 hours**. That will transform your English. Three hours once a month will not. Consistency beats intensity. Never miss two days in a row.

# The 90-Day Fluency Plan

## How the plan works
This plan has **three phases** of 30 days. Every day, spend **45–60 minutes**. Use this book plus listening and speaking practice. Tick each day when you finish it. Don't worry if you need more time – go at your own speed, but **don't stop**.

| Part | Time | What to do |
| Review | 10 min | Read your notebook and yesterday's lesson |
| Learn | 15–20 min | Study the lesson of the day from this book |
| Practise | 10 min | Do the exercises; check the answer key |
| Listen | 10 min | Listen to English (with English subtitles) and shadow |
| Speak / Write | 5–10 min | Speak for 2 minutes on a topic, or write your diary |

## After 90 days: keep going!
- Repeat the parts of this book that were difficult.
- Read one graded reader or simple novel every month.
- Watch series and films with English subtitles, then without.

- Keep your diary. Keep learning 10 words a day.
- Set a new goal: an exam (IELTS, TOEFL, Cambridge), a job, a trip.

# Pronunciation Practice and Tongue Twisters

## Daily pronunciation workout (5 minutes)
1. **Warm up your mouth**: open wide, smile, make an "O" shape, move your tongue around.
2. **Minimal pairs**: say these pairs, feeling the difference.
3. **Tongue twister**: choose one, say it slowly, then faster.
4. **Sentence stress**: read a sentence and stress the important words.

## Minimal pairs
| Sound | Pairs |

| /æ/ – /e/ | bad – bed, man – men, sat – set, had – head, pan – pen |
| /æ/ – /ʌ/ | cat – cut, bag – bug, hat – hut, match – much, ran – run |

## Tongue twisters
Start slowly. Repeat three times. Then speed up.
- She sells seashells by the seashore.
- Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.
- How much wood would a woodchuck chuck if a woodchuck could chuck wood?
- Red lorry, yellow lorry. (repeat 5 times)
- Unique New York, unique New York, you know you need unique New York.
- I scream, you scream, we all scream for ice cream.
- Three free throws. (th – fr – thr)
- The thirty-three thieves thought that they thrilled the throne throughout Thursday.
- Fred fed Ted bread, and Ted fed Fred bread.
- Very well, very well, very well. (v – w)
- We surely shall see the sun shine soon.
- Betty Botter bought some butter, but she said, "This butter's bitter."
- A big black bug bit a big black bear.
- Six slippery snails slid slowly seaward.
- Willie's really weary. (w – r)
- Which witch is which?
- Lesser leather never weathered wetter weather better.
- Truly rural, truly rural.
- A proper copper coffee pot.
- Eleven benevolent elephants.

## Sentence stress practice
Read these. Stress the **CAPITAL** words. Small words are quick and weak.
- I'd LIKE a CUP of TEA, PLEASE.
- WHAT are you DOING at the WEEKEND?
- She's GOING to LONDON NEXT WEEK.
- I've NEVER BEEN to JAPAN.
- CAN you HELP me with my BAGS?
- I DIDN'T KNOW you were COMING!
- It's the BEST FILM I've EVER SEEN.
### Stress changes meaning
- **I** didn't say he stole the money. (someone else said it)
- I didn't **say** he stole the money. (I wrote it, maybe!)
- I didn't say **he** stole the money. (someone else stole it)
- I didn't say he **stole** the money. (he borrowed it)
- I didn't say he stole the **money**. (he stole something else)

# Final Test: Check Your Progress

## Instructions
This test covers the whole book. Do it **without looking** at the lessons. Then check the answer key. Give yourself one point for each correct answer.
- **45–50**: Excellent! You have a strong foundation. Focus on speaking and listening practice.
- **35–44**: Very good. Review the topics where you made mistakes.
- **25–34**: Good start. Go back to Parts 2–4 and review them carefully.
- **Below 25**: Don't worry! Repeat the 90-day plan more slowly. You will improve.

### Writing

### Grammar
- I can use the present simple and continuous correctly.
