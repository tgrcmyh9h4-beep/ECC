# Greetings, Introductions and Goodbyes

## Saying hello
| Formal | Informal |
| Good morning. (until 12) | Hi! |
| Good afternoon. (12–5 p.m.) | Hello! |
| Good evening. (after 5 p.m.) | Hey! (very informal) |
| How do you do? (very formal, first meeting) | Hi there! |
| Pleased to meet you. / Nice to meet you. | Good to see you! |
| It's a pleasure to meet you. | Long time no see! (haven't seen you for a long time) |
> **Tip:** "Good night" is **not** a greeting. It means goodbye, at night or before sleeping.

## How are you? – and how to answer
| Question | Answers |
| How are you? | I'm fine, thanks. And you? |
| How are you doing? | I'm good / great / very well, thank you. |
| How's it going? | Not bad. / Pretty good. / Can't complain. |
| How have you been? | I've been busy, but good. |
| What's up? (informal) | Not much. / Nothing much. You? |
| How's everything? | Everything's fine. |
| How's work? / How's your family? | It's going well. / They're all well, thanks. |
If you're not fine: "Not so good, actually. I've got a cold." / "I'm a bit tired today."
> **Tip:** "How are you?" is often just a greeting. People expect a short, positive answer and then "And you?" Always ask back!

## Introducing yourself
- Hi, I'm Rohan. / My name is Rohan Mehta.
- I'm from Jaipur, in Rajasthan.
- I live in Bangalore now.
- I'm 26 years old.
- I work as a software engineer. / I'm a student. I'm studying commerce.
- I work for an IT company.
- I'm married with two kids. / I'm single.
- In my free time, I like reading and playing badminton.
- I'm learning English because I want to work abroad.
### A full example
"Hello everyone. My name is Fatima and I'm 24. I'm from Hyderabad, but I live in Pune now because of my job. I work as a nurse in a big hospital. I live with my sister. In my free time, I love cooking and watching films. I'm learning English because I want to improve my career and travel to new countries. Nice to meet you all!"

## Introducing other people
- This is my friend, Ali.
- I'd like you to meet my colleague, Ms Sharma.
- Let me introduce you to my boss, Mr Das.
- Have you met my sister, Neha?
Reply: "Nice to meet you." / "Pleased to meet you." / "Hi, Ali. Good to meet you."

## Asking about someone
| Question | Example answer |
| What's your name? | I'm Kiran. |
| Where are you from? | I'm from Nepal. |
| Where do you live? | I live in Kathmandu. |
| What do you do? | I'm an accountant. |
| Where do you work? | At a bank in the city centre. |
| How old are you? (careful – can be personal) | I'm 30. |
| Are you married? (personal) | Yes, I am. / No, I'm single. |
| Do you have any children? | Yes, a son and a daughter. |
| What do you do in your free time? | I play guitar. |
| How long have you lived here? | For about three years. |
| Why are you learning English? | For my job. |
> **Tip:** In many English-speaking cultures, questions about **age, salary, weight, religion and politics** can be personal. Be careful with them when you first meet someone.

## Saying goodbye
| Formal | Informal |
| Goodbye. | Bye! / Bye-bye! |
| It was nice meeting you. | See you! / See you later! |
| Have a nice day. | See you soon! / See you tomorrow! |
| I look forward to seeing you again. | Take care! |
| Good night. (at night) | Catch you later! |
| | Talk to you soon. |
### Ending a conversation politely
- "Well, it was great talking to you, but I have to go now."
- "Sorry, I need to leave. I have a meeting."
- "Let's keep in touch!"
- "Say hello to your family for me."

# Small Talk: Easy Conversation Topics

## What is small talk?
Small talk is short, friendly conversation about easy topics. People use it with neighbours, colleagues, people at parties, taxi drivers – anyone! Good small talk helps you make friends and feel confident.
### Safe topics
- the weather
- the weekend / holidays
- work or studies (in general)
- food and restaurants
- films, TV series, music, sports
- travel
- the place you are in (the party, the event, the city)
### Topics to avoid with new people
- salary and money
- religion and politics
- age and weight
- very personal problems

## Useful small talk questions
| Topic | Questions |
| Weather | Lovely day, isn't it? / It's so hot today! / Do you think it'll rain? |
| Weekend | Did you have a good weekend? / Any plans for the weekend? / What did you get up to? |
| Work | How's work going? / Have you been busy? / How long have you worked here? |
| Free time | What do you like doing in your free time? / Do you play any sports? |
| Food | Have you tried the new restaurant? / What's your favourite food? |
| Films / TV | Have you seen any good films recently? / Are you watching anything good? |
| Travel | Have you been on holiday this year? / Where would you love to travel? |
| Place | How do you know the host? / Is this your first time here? / How long have you lived in this city? |

## How to keep the conversation going
### 1. Ask follow-up questions
A: "I went to Goa last weekend."
B: "Oh, nice! **Who did you go with?** / **How was it?** / **What did you do there?**"
### 2. Don't answer only "yes" or "no" – add information
A: "Do you like your job?"
✗ "Yes."
✓ "Yes, I do. It's busy, but my colleagues are great. What about you?"
### 3. Show interest
| Phrase | When |
| Really? / Oh, really? | surprise, interest |
| That's interesting! | |
| Wow! / That's amazing! | |
| Oh, I see. / I see what you mean. | understanding |
| That sounds great / fun / lovely. | positive news |
| Oh no! / That's terrible! / I'm sorry to hear that. | bad news |
| Lucky you! | someone has something good |
| Me too! / Same here! | you agree |
| Good for you! | someone did something good |
| No way! | very surprised (informal) |
### 4. Ask "What about you?" / "How about you?"
Always give the other person a chance to talk.
### 5. Use "fillers" to give yourself time to think
Native speakers use small words while they think. It's normal and natural!
- Well, ...
- Um... / Er...
- Let me think...
- Let me see...
- You know, ...
- I mean, ...
- Actually, ...
- That's a good question.
- How can I say this?
- What's the word...?
- Kind of / sort of ...

## Example small talk conversation
A: Hi! Lovely weather today, isn't it?
B: Yes, it's beautiful. Finally, no rain!
A: I know! The monsoon was so long this year. Did you do anything fun at the weekend?
B: Not much, actually. I just relaxed at home and watched a series. How about you?
A: I went hiking with some friends near Lonavala.
B: Oh, really? That sounds amazing. How was it?
A: It was great, but very tiring! We walked for six hours.
B: Wow! I'd love to try that. Do you go hiking often?
A: About once a month. You should come with us next time!
B: I'd love to. Thanks!
EX: Choose the best response
1. "I got a new job!" – (a) "Good for you! Congratulations!" (b) "Oh no!" || (a) Good for you! Congratulations!
2. "My cat died last week." – (a) "Lucky you!" (b) "Oh, I'm so sorry to hear that." || (b) Oh, I'm so sorry to hear that.
3. "I love Italian food." – (a) "Me too! Have you tried the new pizza place?" (b) "Yes." || (a) Me too! Have you tried the new pizza place?
4. "Did you have a good weekend?" – (a) "Yes." (b) "Yes, it was really relaxing. I visited my parents. And you?" || (b) Yes, it was really relaxing...

# Everyday Functions: Phrases You Need Every Day

## Thanking and responding
| Thanking | Responding |
| Thanks. / Thank you. | You're welcome. |
| Thanks a lot. / Thank you very much. | No problem. / No worries. |
| Thank you so much for your help. | My pleasure. / Don't mention it. |
| I really appreciate it. | Not at all. / Anytime! |
| That's very kind of you. | Glad I could help. |
| Cheers! (UK, informal) | |

## Apologising and responding
| Apologising | Responding |
| Sorry. | That's OK. / No problem. |
| I'm (so / really) sorry. | Don't worry about it. |
| I'm sorry I'm late. | It doesn't matter. |
| I apologise for the mistake. (formal) | Never mind. |
| Excuse me. (before interrupting or passing someone) | That's all right. |
| Pardon me. / Sorry? (didn't hear) | Apology accepted. |
| My fault. / My bad. (informal) | It's fine. Forget it. |
| I didn't mean to. | |
> **Tip:** Use "**Excuse me**" **before** you disturb someone ("Excuse me, can I ask you something?"). Use "**Sorry**" **after** you make a mistake ("Sorry, I stepped on your foot!").

## Making requests
| More polite ↑ | |
| Would you mind opening the window? | (answer "No, not at all" = yes I'll do it!) |
| Could you possibly help me? | |
| Could you help me, please? | |
| Can you help me? | |
| Help me, please. | (less polite) |
Asking for permission:
- **Can I** use your phone?
- **Could I** sit here?
- **May I** come in? (formal)
- **Do you mind if I** open the window?
- **Is it OK if I** leave early today?
Answers: "Sure." / "Of course." / "Go ahead." / "No problem." / "I'm afraid not." / "Sorry, you can't."

## Offering and responding
| Offering | Accepting | Refusing |
| Would you like some tea? | Yes, please. / I'd love some. | No, thank you. / I'm fine, thanks. |
| Can I help you? | That would be great, thanks. | No, it's OK, thanks. |
| Shall I carry your bag? | That's very kind of you. | Don't worry, I can manage. |
| Let me get that for you. | Thanks a lot! | It's all right, thank you. |
| How about some cake? | Sounds good! | Not for me, thanks. |

## Invitations
| Inviting | Accepting | Refusing politely |
| Would you like to come to my party? | I'd love to! | I'd love to, but I'm busy. |
| Do you want to go for a coffee? | Sure, why not? | Sorry, I can't. Maybe next time? |
| Are you free on Saturday? | Yes, I am. What's the plan? | I'm afraid I have other plans. |
| How about dinner tonight? | That sounds great! | Thanks for asking, but I can't tonight. |
| Let's go to the cinema! | Good idea! | Can I take a rain check? |
> **Tip:** When you say no to an invitation, always give a reason and say thank you. Just "No" sounds rude in English.

## Suggestions
- **Let's** go to the beach.
- **Why don't we** order pizza?
- **How about** watching a film?
- **What about** going for a walk?
- **Shall we** start?
- **We could** take a taxi.
- **Maybe we should** leave now.
- **If I were you, I'd** take the job.

## Agreeing and disagreeing
| Agreeing | Partly agreeing | Disagreeing (politely) |
| I agree. | Maybe, but... | I'm not sure about that. |
| Exactly! / Absolutely! | That's true, but... | I see your point, but... |
| You're right. | I agree up to a point. | I don't really agree. |
| That's a good point. | Yes, but on the other hand... | I'm afraid I disagree. |
| I think so too. | I see what you mean, but... | I see it differently. |
| Definitely. | It depends. | Actually, I think... |
| Me too. / Same here. | | Really? I think... |
! I am agree with you. => I agree with you.
! I am not agree. => I **don't** agree. / I disagree.

## Expressing likes and dislikes
| Love (++) | Like (+) | Neutral | Dislike (–) | Hate (– –) |
| I love... | I like... | I don't mind... | I don't like... | I hate... |
| I'm crazy about... | I'm into... | It's OK. | I'm not keen on... | I can't stand... |
| I adore... | I enjoy... | | I'm not a big fan of... | I really dislike... |
| ...is my favourite. | I'm fond of... | | | ...drives me crazy. |
- "I'**m really into** photography."
- "I **can't stand** waiting in queues."

## Talking about ability
- I can swim well. / I can't drive.
- I'm good at maths. / I'm not very good at singing.
- I'm quite good at cooking.
- I'm terrible at remembering names!
- I know how to use Excel.

| Situation | Phrases |
| Good news | That's great! / Congratulations! / I'm so happy for you! / Well done! |
| Bad news | I'm sorry to hear that. / That's terrible. / Oh no! / What a shame! / That's too bad. |

| Annoyed | That's so annoying! / I'm fed up with this. / This is ridiculous! |
