# Past Simple

## What is it?
The past simple is for actions that **started and finished in the past**, usually at a known time.
- I **visited** my grandmother **yesterday**.
- She **bought** a new car **last month**.
- We **didn't go** to school **on Monday**.

## How to make it
### Positive: verb + -ed (regular) or V2 (irregular)
The form is the **same for all persons** (I, you, he, she, we, they). No "-s"!
| Regular | Irregular |
| I work**ed** | I **went** |
| She play**ed** | She **ate** |
| They stud**ied** | They **saw** |
| We stop**ped** | We **had** |
### Negative: didn't + base verb
- I **didn't work** yesterday.
- She **didn't go** to the party.
### Questions: Did + subject + base verb?
| Question | Short answers |
| **Did** you **see** the match? | Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. |
| **Did** she **call** you? | Yes, she did. / No, she didn't. |
| **Where did** you **go**? | I went to Goa. |
| **What did** they **eat**? | They ate pizza. |
| **When did** he **arrive**? | He arrived at 9. |
! I didn't went. => I didn't **go**.
! Did you saw him? => Did you **see** him?
! She didn't called me. => She didn't **call** me.
! Yesterday I go to the market. => Yesterday I **went** to the market.
! I goed / I eated / I buyed => I **went** / I **ate** / I **bought**
> **Tip:** After "did" and "didn't", the verb always returns to the **base form**. The "did" already shows the past. You don't need to show the past twice!

## When to use it
### 1. Finished actions at a definite time
- I **met** her **in 2015**.
- They **arrived** **two hours ago**.
- **Last night** we **watched** a film.
Signal words: yesterday, last night/week/month/year, ago, in 2010, when I was a child, then, after that
### 2. A sequence of past actions (a story)
- I **woke up**, **had** a shower, **got** dressed and **left** for work.
### 3. Past habits
- When I was a child, I **played** in the street every day.
### 4. A finished period of time
- He **lived** in Paris for ten years. (He doesn't live there now.)

## "Ago"
"Ago" means "back from now". It goes **after** the time period.
- I came here **three years ago**.
- She called **ten minutes ago**.
- How long **ago** did you start?
! I came before two years. => I came two years **ago**.
! Ago two days => two days **ago**

## A short story in the past simple
Read the story aloud.
"Last Saturday **was** a terrible day for Arjun. He **woke up** late because his alarm **didn't ring**. He **jumped** out of bed and **ran** to the bus stop, but the bus **left** just before he **arrived**. He **took** a taxi, but there **was** a lot of traffic. When he finally **got** to the office, he **realised** something: it **was** Saturday! The office **was** closed. He **laughed**, **bought** a coffee and **went** back home to sleep."
### Questions about the story
- Why did Arjun wake up late? – Because his alarm didn't ring.
- Did he catch the bus? – No, he didn't.
- How did he go to the office? – He took a taxi.
- Why was the office closed? – Because it was Saturday.

# Past Continuous

## What is it?
The past continuous is for an action that was **in progress** at a time in the past.
- At 8 o'clock last night, I **was watching** TV.
- What **were** you **doing** when I called?

## How to make it: was / were + verb-ing
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / He / She / It **was** working | I **wasn't** working | **Was** she working? |
| You / We / They **were** working | They **weren't** working | **Were** you working? |

## When to use it
### 1. An action in progress at a specific past time
- At 7 a.m. I **was having** breakfast.
- This time last year, we **were living** in Bangalore.
### 2. A long action interrupted by a short action
The long action → past continuous. The short action → past simple.
- I **was cooking** when the phone **rang**.
- She **was walking** home when she **saw** an accident.
- While we **were sleeping**, someone **broke** into the house.
### 3. Two long actions at the same time (while)
- **While** I **was studying**, my brother **was playing** video games.
### 4. Background description in a story
- It **was raining**. People **were hurrying** home. A dog **was barking** somewhere...

## When or while?
- **when** + past simple (short action): "I was reading **when** she **arrived**."
- **while** + past continuous (long action): "She arrived **while** I **was reading**."
! When I was sleep, the phone rang. => When I **was sleeping**, the phone rang.
! I was knowing the answer. => I **knew** the answer. (stative verb)
EX: Complete with past simple or past continuous
1. I ___ (read) when the lights ___ (go) out. || was reading ... went
2. What ___ you ___ (do) at 9 last night? || were ... doing
3. She ___ (cut) her finger while she ___ (cook). || cut ... was cooking
4. We ___ (watch) TV when you ___ (call). || were watching ... called
5. While they ___ (play) football, it ___ (start) to rain. || were playing ... started
6. I ___ (see) Priya while I ___ (wait) for the bus. || saw ... was waiting
7. It ___ (snow) when we ___ (wake up). || was snowing ... woke up
8. He ___ (not / listen) when the teacher ___ (explain) the lesson. || wasn't listening ... was explaining

# Past Perfect

## What is it?
The past perfect is "the past of the past". We use it to show that one past action happened **before** another past action.
- When I arrived at the station, the train **had** already **left**.
(First: the train left. Second: I arrived.)

## How to make it: had + past participle (V3)
The form is the same for all persons.
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / She / They **had** (I'd) finished | I **hadn't** finished | **Had** you finished? |

## When to use it
### 1. The earlier of two past actions
- She **had cooked** dinner before her husband came home.
- When we got to the cinema, the film **had** already **started**.
- I **had** never **seen** the sea before I went to Goa.
### 2. Reported speech (see Part 4)
- He said he **had lost** his phone.
### 3. Third conditional (see Part 4)
- If I **had studied**, I would have passed.
### Compare
- When I arrived, she **left**. (I arrived, then she left.)
- When I arrived, she **had left**. (She left first. I didn't see her.)
> **Tip:** You don't need the past perfect if "before" or "after" already makes the order clear: "I ate dinner before I went out" is fine.
EX: Complete with the past perfect
1. When I got home, my family ___ (eat) dinner. || had eaten
2. She was sad because she ___ (lose) her ring. || had lost
3. I ___ (never / fly) before that trip. || had never flown
4. By the time the police came, the thief ___ (escape). || had escaped
5. ___ you ___ (finish) the work before the manager arrived? || Had ... finished
6. He ___ (not / study), so he failed the exam. || hadn't studied

# Past Perfect Continuous

## What is it?
The past perfect continuous shows that an action **continued for some time before** another past moment.
- I **had been waiting** for two hours when the bus finally came.
- She was tired because she **had been working** all day.

## How to make it: had been + verb-ing
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / He / They **had been** working | I **hadn't been** working | **Had** you **been** working? |

## When to use it
### 1. Duration before a past point
- They **had been living** in London for ten years before they moved to Canada.
- How long **had** you **been studying** English before you went to Australia?
### 2. Cause of a past situation
- His eyes were red because he **had been crying**.
- The ground was wet. It **had been raining**.
EX: Complete with the past perfect continuous
1. I was tired because I ___ (run). || had been running
2. They ___ (wait) for an hour when the doctor came. || had been waiting
3. She ___ (work) there for five years before she got a promotion. || had been working
4. The road was wet. It ___ (rain). || had been raining
5. How long ___ he ___ (drive) before he stopped? || had ... been driving

# Used To and Would (Past Habits)

## Used to
We use **used to + base verb** for past habits or states that are **not true now**.
- I **used to** play cricket every day. (but I don't now)
- She **used to** live in Mumbai. (she lives somewhere else now)
- There **used to** be a cinema here. (now there isn't)
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I **used to** smoke. | I **didn't use to** smoke. | **Did** you **use to** smoke? |
! I use to play cricket. (past) => I **used to** play cricket.
! Did you used to live here? => Did you **use to** live here?
! I am used to play football when I was young. => I **used to** play football when I was young.

## Be used to / get used to (different meaning!)
**be used to + -ing / noun** = be familiar with, it is normal for me.
- I **am used to** waking up early. (It's normal for me – not difficult.)
- She **isn't used to** the cold weather.
**get used to + -ing / noun** = become familiar with.
- You will **get used to** driving on the left.
- It took me a month to **get used to** my new job.
| Structure | Meaning | Example |
| used to + verb | past habit | I **used to** work at night. |
| be used to + -ing | normal for me | I'**m used to working** at night. |
| get used to + -ing | becoming normal | I'**m getting used to working** at night. |

## Would (past repeated actions)
"Would" can also describe repeated past actions (not states), often in stories about childhood.
- Every summer, we **would** go to my grandparents' village. My grandmother **would** make sweets for us.
! I would have long hair when I was young. (state) => I **used to** have long hair.
EX: Complete with used to / didn't use to / use to
1. I ___ live in a village, but now I live in the city. || used to
2. She ___ like vegetables, but now she loves them. || didn't use to
3. Did you ___ play with dolls? || use to
4. There ___ be a park here. Now it's a car park. || used to
5. My father ___ smoke, but he stopped five years ago. || used to
EX: Choose the correct form
1. I'm not used to (get / getting) up so early. || getting
2. He used to (work / working) in a bank. || work
3. You'll soon get used to (live / living) here. || living
4. When I was a child, I (would / used to) have a dog. || used to
