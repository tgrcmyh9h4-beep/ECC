# Parts of Speech: The 8 Word Families

## Why learn parts of speech?
Every English word belongs to a family. When you know the family, you know **where to put the word** in a sentence. This is the secret of good grammar.
| Part of speech | What it does | Examples |
| Noun | names a person, place, thing or idea | teacher, Mumbai, phone, love |
| Pronoun | takes the place of a noun | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| Verb | shows an action or a state | run, eat, think, is, have |
| Adjective | describes a noun | big, happy, red, beautiful |
| Adverb | describes a verb, adjective or another adverb | quickly, very, always, well |
| Preposition | shows place, time or direction | in, on, at, under, before |
| Conjunction | joins words or sentences | and, but, or, because, so |
| Interjection | shows a sudden feeling | Wow! Oh! Ouch! Hey! |

## Look at one sentence
"**Wow**, my **young** brother **quickly** **ate** **three** **apples** **and** a banana **in** the kitchen."
| Word | Part of speech |
| Wow | interjection |
| my | possessive adjective (determiner) |
| young | adjective |
| brother | noun |
| quickly | adverb |
| ate | verb |
| three | number (determiner) |
| apples | noun |
| and | conjunction |
| in | preposition |
| kitchen | noun |

## Basic English word order: S + V + O
English has a **fixed word order**. This is very important. The basic order is:
**Subject + Verb + Object** (+ Place + Time)
| Subject (who?) | Verb (action) | Object (what?) | Place (where?) | Time (when?) |
| I | eat | rice | at home | every day. |
| She | watched | a film | at the cinema | yesterday. |
| We | will visit | our grandparents | in Pune | next week. |
| They | play | football | in the park | on Sundays. |
! I rice eat. => I eat rice.
! Yesterday a film she watched. => She watched a film yesterday. (or: Yesterday, she watched a film.)
> **Tip:** Remember "**S-V-O-P-T**": Subject, Verb, Object, Place, Time. Place usually comes before time.

## Every sentence needs a subject and a verb
In some languages, you can drop the subject. In English, you almost always need it.
! Is raining. => **It** is raining.
! Is very hot today. => **It** is very hot today.
! Am happy. => **I** am happy.
! My brother very tall. => My brother **is** very tall.

## One word, many families
The same root can change its family by adding an ending (suffix):
| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
| happiness | – | happy | happily |
| beauty | beautify | beautiful | beautifully |
| success | succeed | successful | successfully |
| decision | decide | decisive | decisively |
| care | care | careful | carefully |
| danger | endanger | dangerous | dangerously |
| education | educate | educational | educationally |
| strength | strengthen | strong | strongly |
| speed | speed | quick / fast | quickly / fast |
| difference | differ | different | differently |
### Common endings help you guess the family
| Ending | Usually a... | Examples |
| -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -er, -or | noun | information, decision, payment, kindness, city, teacher, actor |
| -ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -able, -al, -ic, -y | adjective | helpful, useless, famous, active, comfortable, national, basic, sunny |
| -ly | adverb (usually) | slowly, really, happily |
| -ize, -ise, -en, -fy, -ate | verb | organize, widen, simplify, create |

1. She is a **doctor**. || noun
2. They **run** every morning. || verb
3. It is a **beautiful** day. || adjective
4. He speaks **slowly**. || adverb
5. The cat is **under** the table. || preposition
6. I like tea **but** I don't like coffee. || conjunction

# Nouns: Singular, Plural, Countable, Uncountable

## What is a noun?
A noun is a name. It can be a person (**girl, doctor, Amit**), a place (**school, London, park**), a thing (**book, car, water**), an animal (**dog, bird**) or an idea (**love, freedom, time**).
### Common and proper nouns
- **Common nouns** are general names: city, river, boy, month. Write them with a small letter.
- **Proper nouns** are special names: Delhi, the Ganges, Rahul, January. Always start them with a **capital letter**.
! I live in mumbai. => I live in **M**umbai.
! My birthday is in july. => My birthday is in **J**uly.
> **Tip:** In English we use capital letters for days (Monday), months (March), languages (English, Hindi), nationalities (Indian, French) and the word "I".

## Making plurals
### The normal rule: add -s
book → books, car → cars, friend → friends, apple → apples
### Add -es after s, ss, sh, ch, x, z (and some words ending in o)
| Singular | Plural |
| bus | buses |
| class | classes |
| dish | dishes |
| watch | watches |
| box | boxes |
| tomato | tomatoes |
| potato | potatoes |
| hero | heroes |
But: photo → photos, piano → pianos, radio → radios, video → videos.
### Consonant + y → -ies
baby → babies, city → cities, country → countries, party → parties, story → stories
But vowel + y → just add -s: boy → boys, day → days, key → keys, toy → toys
### -f / -fe → -ves
leaf → leaves, knife → knives, wife → wives, life → lives, half → halves, wolf → wolves, shelf → shelves
But: roof → roofs, chef → chefs, belief → beliefs
### Irregular plurals – learn these by heart!
| Singular | Plural |
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| child | children |
| person | people |
| foot | feet |
| tooth | teeth |
| mouse | mice |
| goose | geese |
| ox | oxen |
### Same in singular and plural
sheep → sheep, fish → fish, deer → deer, series → series, species → species
### Always plural
trousers, jeans, shorts, glasses (for eyes), scissors, clothes, police
- My jeans **are** blue. (not "is")
- The police **are** here.
- I need **a pair of** scissors.

## Countable and uncountable nouns
This is one of the most important topics in English grammar.
**Countable nouns** can be counted: one apple, two apples, three apples. They have a singular and plural form.
**Uncountable nouns** cannot be counted with numbers. They have **no plural**. We cannot use "a/an" with them.
| Countable | Uncountable |
| a chair, two chairs | furniture |
| a suitcase, bags | luggage / baggage |
| a coin, a note | money |
| a job | work |
| a suggestion, a tip | advice |
| a fact, a news story | information, news |
| a word | vocabulary |
| a song | music |
| a loaf, a slice | bread |
| a meal | food |
| a car | traffic |
| a trip, a journey | travel |
### More uncountable nouns
- **Liquids**: water, milk, juice, tea, coffee, oil
- **Materials**: wood, paper, glass, plastic, gold
- **Food**: rice, sugar, salt, bread, cheese, butter, meat
- **Ideas**: love, happiness, time, help, knowledge, patience, fun
- **Other**: homework, equipment, research, weather, hair, electricity
! I need an advice. => I need **some advice**. / I need **a piece of** advice.
! She gave me many informations. => She gave me **a lot of information**.
! The news are bad. => The news **is** bad.
! I have a lot of homeworks. => I have a lot of **homework**.
! We bought new furnitures. => We bought new **furniture**.
! His hairs are long. => His **hair is** long.
### How to "count" uncountable nouns
Use a container or unit word:
| Unit | Examples |
| a glass of | water, milk, juice |
| a cup of | tea, coffee |
| a bottle of | water, oil, wine |
| a piece of | advice, information, paper, cake, furniture |
| a slice of | bread, pizza, cheese |
| a loaf of | bread |
| a kilo of | rice, sugar, meat |
| a bowl of | rice, soup |
| a packet of | biscuits, crisps |
| a bar of | chocolate, soap |
| a spoonful of | sugar, salt |
| a drop of | water, rain |

## Possessive nouns ('s)
Use **'s** to show that something belongs to someone.
- Riya's bag (the bag of Riya)
- my father's car
- the teacher's desk
For plural nouns ending in -s, add only an apostrophe ('):
- my parents' house (two parents)
- the students' books
For irregular plurals, add 's: the children's toys, the women's room, people's opinions.
! The car of my father is new. => My father's car is new.
> **Tip:** Use 's mainly for people and animals. For things, we often use "of": the end **of** the film, the top **of** the page, the name **of** the street.

# Articles: A, An and The

## What are articles?
Articles are small words that come before nouns. English has three: **a**, **an** and **the**. Many languages do not have articles, so learners often forget them. Practise them carefully – they appear in almost every sentence!

## A and An (the indefinite article)
Use **a** or **an** with a **singular countable noun** when:
- you talk about it for the **first time**: "I saw **a** dog in the street."
- it is **one of many** / not a specific one: "Can I borrow **a** pen?"
- you say someone's **job**: "She is **a** nurse." "He is **an** engineer."
- you describe something: "It's **a** beautiful day."
### A or an?
The rule is about the **sound**, not the letter.
- **a** + consonant **sound**: a book, a car, **a university** (you-ni...), **a European** country, **a one**-way street
- **an** + vowel **sound**: an apple, an egg, an idea, an orange, an umbrella, **an hour** (h is silent), **an honest** man, **an MBA** (em-bee-ay)
! She is engineer. => She is **an** engineer.
! I have a umbrella. => I have **an** umbrella.
! He is an university student. => He is **a** university student.

## The (the definite article)
Use **the** when the listener knows **which one** you mean.
- When you mention something **again**: "I saw a dog. **The** dog was very big."
- When there is **only one**: **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** world, **the** sky, **the** Internet
- When it is clear from the situation: "Close **the** door, please." (the door of this room)
- With superlatives: **the** best, **the** tallest, **the** most important
- With ordinal numbers: **the** first, **the** second, **the** last
- With some places: **the** cinema, **the** theatre, **the** bank, **the** post office, **the** station
- With musical instruments: play **the** guitar, play **the** piano
- With rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges: **the** Ganges, **the** Pacific Ocean, **the** Himalayas
- With some countries: **the** USA, **the** UK, **the** Netherlands, **the** Philippines
- With plural family names: **the** Sharmas (the Sharma family)

## No article (zero article)
Do **not** use an article:
- With plural or uncountable nouns used in a general sense: "**Dogs** are friendly." "I love **music**." "**Water** is important."
- With names of people, most cities and countries: **Priya**, **Paris**, **India**, **Japan**
- With meals: have **breakfast**, have **lunch**, have **dinner**
- With languages and school subjects: I speak **English**. I study **maths**.
- With sports: play **cricket**, play **football**
- With days, months, years: on **Monday**, in **June**, in **2026**
- In some fixed phrases: go to **school**, go to **work**, go to **bed**, go **home**, at **night**, by **bus**, by **car**, in **hospital** (UK)
! I love the music. (meaning music in general) => I love music.
! The life is beautiful. => Life is beautiful.
! I go to the home at 6. => I go home at 6.
! She plays guitar very well. (US accepts this, but standard is) => She plays **the** guitar very well.
! I had a lunch with my friend. => I had lunch with my friend.

## General or specific?
Compare:
| General (no article) | Specific (the) |
| I like **coffee**. | **The coffee** in this café is excellent. |
| **Children** need love. | **The children** in my class are noisy. |
| **Money** isn't everything. | Where is **the money** I gave you? |
| **Books** are expensive. | **The books** on the table are mine. |

## School, hospital, prison, church
- "He goes to **school**." = he is a student (the normal purpose).
- "His mother went to **the school** to talk to the teacher." = she visited the building.
- "She is in **hospital**." (UK) = she is a patient.
- "I went to **the hospital** to visit her." = visiting the building.

## Article summary table
| Situation | Article | Example |
| First mention, singular | a / an | I bought **a** shirt. |
| Second mention | the | **The** shirt is blue. |
| Only one exists | the | **The** moon is bright. |

| General plural | – | **Cats** like milk. |
| General uncountable | – | **Sugar** is sweet. |
| Names, cities, countries | – | **Ravi** lives in **Chennai**. |
| Superlatives | the | the best day |
| Meals, sports, languages | – | have **dinner**, play **tennis**, learn **French** |

# Pronouns and Possessives

## Subject and object pronouns
Pronouns take the place of nouns, so we do not repeat words.
"Ali is my friend. **Ali** lives near me." → "Ali is my friend. **He** lives near me."
| Subject pronoun | Object pronoun | Possessive adjective | Possessive pronoun | Reflexive pronoun |
| I | me | my | mine | myself |
| you | you | your | yours | yourself / yourselves |
| he | him | his | his | himself |
| she | her | her | hers | herself |
| it | it | its | – | itself |
| we | us | our | ours | ourselves |
| they | them | their | theirs | themselves |

## Subject pronouns
Use them **before** the verb – they do the action.
- **I** like tea.
- **She** works in a bank.
- **They** are my cousins.
- **It** is raining. (We use "it" for weather, time and distance: "It's 5 o'clock." "It's 10 km to the city.")

## Object pronouns
Use them **after** the verb or **after a preposition** – they receive the action.
- Call **me** tomorrow.
- I love **her**.
- Can you help **us**?
- This gift is for **you**.
- Come with **them**.
! Me and my friend went shopping. => My friend and **I** went shopping.
! She gave the book to I. => She gave the book to **me**.
! Between you and I... => Between you and **me**...

## Possessive adjectives and pronouns
**Possessive adjectives** come **before a noun**: my book, your car, her phone, our house, their dog.
**Possessive pronouns** stand **alone** (no noun after them): The book is **mine**. Is this pen **yours**? The red car is **hers**.
- This is **my** bag. → This bag is **mine**.
- That is **their** house. → That house is **theirs**.
! This is mine bag. => This is **my** bag.
! The phone is her. => The phone is **hers**.
### Its or it's?
- **its** = possessive: "The dog wagged **its** tail."
- **it's** = it is / it has: "**It's** cold today." "**It's** been a long day."
### Your or you're?
- **your** = possessive: "Is this **your** pen?"
- **you're** = you are: "**You're** very kind."
### Their, there or they're?
- **their** = possessive: "**Their** house is big."
- **there** = place / there is: "Put it **there**." "**There** is a problem."
- **they're** = they are: "**They're** coming at 6."

## Reflexive pronouns
Use them when the subject and the object are the **same person**:
- I cut **myself** while cooking.
- She taught **herself** to play the guitar.
- Enjoy **yourselves** at the party!
- He looked at **himself** in the mirror.
Also for emphasis: "I made this cake **myself**." (nobody helped me)
"**by myself**" = alone: "I live **by myself**."
! He hurt hisself. => He hurt **himself**.
! They enjoyed theirselves. => They enjoyed **themselves**.

## Demonstratives: this, that, these, those
| | Near (here) | Far (there) |
| Singular | this book | that book |
| Plural | these books | those books |
- **This** is my brother. (he is next to me)
- Look at **that** bird! (it is far away)
- **These** shoes are comfortable.
- Who are **those** people?
On the phone: "Hello, **this is** Sara." (not "I am Sara")

## Indefinite pronouns
| | Person | Thing | Place |
| some- | someone / somebody | something | somewhere |
| any- | anyone / anybody | anything | anywhere |
| no- | no one / nobody | nothing | nowhere |
| every- | everyone / everybody | everything | everywhere |
- **Someone** is at the door.
- Is there **anything** to eat?
- **Nobody** knows the answer.
- **Everyone** is happy. (singular verb: "is", not "are")
- I can't find my keys **anywhere**.
! Nobody don't know. => Nobody knows. (only one negative)
! Everybody are here. => Everybody **is** here.

## Who, whose
- **Who** is that man? (person)
- **Whose** bag is this? (who is the owner?) – "It's Maria's." / "It's mine."
! Who's bag is this? => **Whose** bag is this?

# The Verb "To Be"

## Why "be" is so important
"Be" is the most common verb in English. We use it to say who we are, what we are like, where we are, our age, our feelings and much more. It is also used to make other tenses (I am working, it was made).

## Present: am, is, are
| Positive | Short form | Negative | Short form |
| I am | I'm | I am not | I'm not |
| You are | You're | You are not | You aren't / You're not |
| He is | He's | He is not | He isn't / He's not |
| She is | She's | She is not | She isn't / She's not |
| It is | It's | It is not | It isn't / It's not |
| We are | We're | We are not | We aren't / We're not |
| They are | They're | They are not | They aren't / They're not |
> **Tip:** In speaking, we almost always use short forms: "I'm tired." "She's at home." "They aren't here." This sounds natural.

## Questions with be
Change the order: **Be + subject + ...?**
| Question | Short answer: yes | Short answer: no |
| Am I late? | Yes, you are. | No, you aren't. |
| Are you a student? | Yes, I am. | No, I'm not. |
| Is he your brother? | Yes, he is. | No, he isn't. |
| Is it cold? | Yes, it is. | No, it isn't. |
| Are they at home? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |
! Yes, I'm. => Yes, I **am**. (no short form in positive short answers)
### Wh- questions with be
- **Where** are you from? – I'm from Kerala.
- **What** is your name? – My name is Anjali.
- **How** are you? – I'm fine, thanks.
- **How old** is your son? – He's seven.
- **Who** is that woman? – She's my aunt.
- **Why** are you sad? – Because I lost my phone.
- **When** is your birthday? – It's on 5th May.

## Uses of "be"
| Use | Example |
| Name | I'm Karan. |
| Job | She's a dentist. |
| Nationality / origin | We're Indian. They're from Brazil. |
| Age | I'm 25 (years old). |
| Feelings | He's hungry. I'm nervous. |
| Description | The house is big. You're tall. |
| Place | The keys are on the table. |
| Time / weather | It's 3 o'clock. It's sunny. |
| Price | It's 200 rupees. How much are these shoes? |
! I have 25 years. => I **am** 25 years old.
! I have hungry. => I **am** hungry.
! She is agree with me. => She agrees with me.
! I am understand. => I understand.

## Past: was and were
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I was | I wasn't | Was I...? |
| You were | You weren't | Were you...? |
| He / She / It was | He / She / It wasn't | Was he / she / it...? |
| We were | We weren't | Were we...? |
| They were | They weren't | Were they...? |
- I **was** at home yesterday.
- They **were** very tired after the trip.
- It **wasn't** expensive.
- **Were** you at the party? – Yes, I **was**. / No, I **wasn't**.
- Where **were** you last night?
! They was late. => They **were** late.

## There is / There are
Use **there is / there are** to say that something **exists** or is in a place.
| | Singular / uncountable | Plural |
| Positive | There is (There's) a bank near here. | There are three banks in this street. |
| Negative | There isn't any milk. | There aren't any chairs. |
| Question | Is there a hospital? | Are there any shops? |
| Past | There was a storm. | There were many people. |
| Future | There will be a meeting. | There will be problems. |
! In my city have many parks. => In my city **there are** many parks.
! Have a problem with my computer. => **There is** a problem with my computer.
! There is many cars. => There **are** many cars.

5. We ___ from Nepal. || are

# Have and Have Got

## Have for possession
We use **have** to talk about things we own, family, illnesses and features.
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / You / We / They **have** | I / You / We / They **don't have** | **Do** I / you / we / they **have**...? |
| He / She / It **has** | He / She / It **doesn't have** | **Does** he / she / it **have**...? |
- I **have** two brothers.
- She **has** long hair.
- We **don't have** a car.
- **Does** he **have** a job? – Yes, he **does**. / No, he **doesn't**.
- I **have** a headache.
! She have a new phone. => She **has** a new phone.
! He doesn't has time. => He doesn't **have** time.
! Does she has a sister? => Does she **have** a sister?
> **Tip:** After "does" and "doesn't", the verb always returns to its base form: does + have, doesn't + have.

## Have got (mostly British English, informal)
"Have got" means the same as "have" for possession.
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I've got a cat. | I haven't got a cat. | Have you got a cat? |
| She's got a cold. | She hasn't got a cold. | Has she got a cold? |
## Past: had
- I **had** a bicycle when I was a child.
- **Did** you **have** a good time? – Yes, I **did**.
- We **didn't have** enough money.

## Have for actions (not possession)
"Have" is also used in many fixed expressions. Here it is an action, so we can use continuous tenses ("I'm having lunch").
| Expression | Example |
| have breakfast / lunch / dinner | We have dinner at 8. |
| have a shower / bath | I'm having a shower. |
| have a good time | Have a good time at the party! |
| have a party | We're having a party on Saturday. |
| have a rest / a break | Let's have a break. |
| have a look | Can I have a look? |
| have a chat / a conversation | We had a long chat. |
| have a problem / difficulty | I have a problem with my laptop. |
| have a baby | She had a baby last month. |
| have fun | We had fun at the beach. |

## Have to (obligation)
"Have to" means it is necessary.
- I **have to** go to work at 8.
- She **has to** wear a uniform.
- We **don't have to** pay. It's free. (= it is not necessary)
- **Do** you **have to** work on Saturdays?
- I **had to** wait for an hour. (past)
(See the chapter on modal verbs for more.)
