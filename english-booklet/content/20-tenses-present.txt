# Understanding Tenses: The Big Picture

## What is a tense?
A tense tells us **when** an action happens: in the past, the present or the future. It also tells us **how** we see the action: finished, in progress, or connected to another time.
English has **3 times** × **4 aspects** = **12 tenses**.
| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
| Present | I work | I am working | I have worked | I have been working |
| Past | I worked | I was working | I had worked | I had been working |
| Future | I will work | I will be working | I will have worked | I will have been working |
### What do the four aspects mean?
- **Simple**: a fact, a habit, a complete action. "I work in a bank."
- **Continuous** (be + -ing): an action in progress at a time. "I am working now."
- **Perfect** (have + past participle): an action completed before another time; connects two times. "I have finished."
- **Perfect continuous** (have been + -ing): an action that continued up to a time, with a focus on duration. "I have been working for three hours."
> **Tip:** Don't try to learn all 12 at once. The most important for daily conversation are: **present simple, present continuous, past simple, present perfect, and future (will / going to)**. Master these five first. They make up about 90% of everyday English.

## The three verb forms
Every English verb has three main forms. You must know them!
| Base form (V1) | Past simple (V2) | Past participle (V3) |
| work | worked | worked |
| play | played | played |
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| see | saw | seen |
| write | wrote | written |
Regular verbs add **-ed** for V2 and V3. Irregular verbs change in different ways – see the full list of irregular verbs in Part 5.
Plus the **-ing form**: working, playing, going, eating.
### Spelling of -ing
| Rule | Examples |
| Most verbs: + ing | work → working, eat → eating, go → going |
| Ends in -e: drop e + ing | make → making, write → writing, come → coming |
| One vowel + one consonant (stressed): double + ing | run → running, swim → swimming, sit → sitting, begin → beginning |
| Ends in -ie: ie → ying | lie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying |
| Ends in -ee, -ye, -oe: keep e | see → seeing, agree → agreeing |
### Spelling of -ed
| Rule | Examples |
| Most verbs: + ed | walk → walked, clean → cleaned |
| Ends in -e: + d | live → lived, dance → danced |
| Consonant + y: y → ied | study → studied, cry → cried |
| Vowel + y: + ed | play → played, enjoy → enjoyed |
| One vowel + one consonant (stressed): double + ed | stop → stopped, plan → planned, prefer → preferred |

# Present Simple

## What is it?
The present simple is for **facts, habits, routines and things that are always true**.
- I **drink** tea every morning. (habit)
- Water **boils** at 100°C. (fact)
- She **works** in a hospital. (permanent situation)
- The sun **rises** in the east. (always true)

## How to make it
### Positive
| Subject | Verb |
| I / You / We / They | work, play, live |
| He / She / It | work**s**, play**s**, live**s** |
**The golden rule**: with **he, she, it** (or one person/thing), add **-s** to the verb.
### Spelling of -s
| Rule | Examples |
| Most verbs: + s | works, reads, sleeps |
| Ends in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o: + es | misses, washes, watches, fixes, goes, does |
| Consonant + y: y → ies | study → studies, cry → cries, fly → flies |
| Vowel + y: + s | play → plays, buy → buys |
| Irregular | have → **has** |
### Negative: don't / doesn't + base verb
| Subject | Negative |
| I / You / We / They | **don't** (do not) work |
| He / She / It | **doesn't** (does not) work |
### Questions: Do / Does + subject + base verb?
| Question | Short answers |
| **Do** you **like** pizza? | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. |
| **Does** she **speak** French? | Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. |
| **Do** they **live** here? | Yes, they do. / No, they don't. |
| **Where do** you **work**? | I work in a bank. |
| **What time does** the shop **open**? | It opens at 9. |
! She work in a bank. => She work**s** in a bank.
! He don't like coffee. => He **doesn't** like coffee.
! She doesn't likes coffee. => She doesn't **like** coffee.
! Does he works here? => Does he **work** here?
! You like pizza? (in writing) => **Do** you like pizza?
! Where you live? => Where **do** you live?
> **Tip:** The "-s" goes to only ONE place. In positive sentences it goes on the verb (she work**s**). In negatives and questions it goes on "do" (she **does**n't work, **does** she work?).

## When to use it
### 1. Habits and routines
- I **get up** at 6 o'clock every day.
- She **goes** to the gym three times a week.
- We usually **have** dinner at 8.
Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays, once a week
### 2. Facts and general truths
- Cats **like** fish.
- It **rains** a lot in Kerala.
- The Earth **goes** around the Sun.
### 3. Permanent situations
- My parents **live** in Kolkata.
- He **works** for Google.
### 4. Timetables and schedules (future)
- The train **leaves** at 7:30 tomorrow.
- The film **starts** at 9 p.m.
### 5. Stative verbs (see below)
- I **know** the answer.
- She **loves** her children.

## Stative verbs
Some verbs describe **states** (feelings, thoughts, senses, possession), not actions. We usually do **not** use them in the continuous form.
| Type | Verbs |
| Feelings | love, like, hate, want, need, prefer, wish |
| Thinking | know, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, think (= opinion), agree, suppose |
| Senses | see, hear, smell, taste, seem, look (= seem), sound |
| Possession | have (= own), own, belong, possess |
| Others | be, cost, weigh, contain, depend, matter |
! I am knowing the answer. => I **know** the answer.
! She is wanting a new car. => She **wants** a new car.
! I am understanding you. => I **understand** you.
! This is belonging to me. => This **belongs** to me.

# Present Continuous

## What is it?
The present continuous is for actions happening **now**, around now, temporary situations, and future plans.
- I **am reading** this book now.
- She **is cooking** dinner at the moment.

## How to make it: am / is / are + verb-ing
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I **am** (I'm) work**ing** | I'm **not** working | **Am** I working? |
| You / We / They **are** (you're) working | You **aren't** working | **Are** you working? |
| He / She / It **is** (he's) working | He **isn't** working | **Is** he working? |
- What **are** you **doing**? – I'**m watching** TV.
- **Is** it **raining**? – Yes, it **is**. / No, it **isn't**.
! I watching TV. => I **am** watching TV.
! She is cook dinner. => She is cook**ing** dinner.
! What you are doing? => What **are you** doing?

## When to use it
### 1. Actions happening now
- Be quiet! The baby **is sleeping**.
- Look! It**'s snowing**.
- I can't talk now. I'**m driving**.
Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, Look!, Listen!
### 2. Temporary actions (around now, not exactly at this moment)
- I'**m reading** a great book this week.
- She **is living** with her friend until she finds a flat.
- We **are learning** English this year.
### 3. Changing situations
- The weather **is getting** colder.
- Prices **are rising**.
- Your English **is improving**!
### 4. Future plans and arrangements
- I'**m meeting** Rahul tomorrow at 6.
- We **are flying** to Dubai next Friday.
- What **are** you **doing** this weekend?
### 5. Annoying habits (with always)
- He **is always losing** his keys!
- You'**re always complaining**!

## Present simple or present continuous?
| Present simple | Present continuous |
| Habit / always | Now / temporary |
| I **work** in an office. | I'**m working** from home this week. |
| She **plays** tennis on Sundays. | She'**s playing** tennis right now. |
| It **rains** a lot in July. | It'**s raining** now. Take an umbrella. |
| What **do** you **do**? (= What is your job?) | What **are** you **doing**? (= now) |
| He usually **drinks** tea. | Today he'**s drinking** coffee. |
### Verbs with two meanings
Some stative verbs can be actions with a different meaning:
| State (simple) | Action (continuous) |
| I **think** it's a good idea. (opinion) | I'**m thinking** about my holiday. (using my brain) |
| I **have** a car. (possession) | I'**m having** lunch. (eating) |
| The soup **tastes** good. (quality) | The chef **is tasting** the soup. (action) |
| You **look** tired. (seem) | Why **are** you **looking** at me? (action) |
| I **see** what you mean. (understand) | I'**m seeing** the doctor tomorrow. (meeting) |
EX: Present simple or continuous?
1. Shh! The baby ___ (sleep). || is sleeping
2. I usually ___ (walk) to work. || walk
3. Look! The bus ___ (come). || is coming
4. She ___ (not / eat) meat. She's vegetarian. || doesn't eat
5. What ___ you ___ (do) now? || are ... doing

10. ___ you ___ (like) spicy food? || Do ... like
11. My English ___ (get) better every day. || is getting
12. I ___ (think) you are right. || think

# Present Perfect

## What is it?
The present perfect connects the **past** and the **present**. We use it when the time is **not important or not finished**, or when a past action has a **result now**.
- I **have lost** my keys. (I don't have them now.)
- She **has been** to London three times. (in her life)
- We **have lived** here since 2018. (and we still live here)

## How to make it: have / has + past participle (V3)
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / You / We / They **have** (I've) finished | I **haven't** finished | **Have** you finished? |
| He / She / It **has** (she's) finished | She **hasn't** finished | **Has** she finished? |
- **Have** you **eaten**? – Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven't**.
- She **has gone** home.
- They **haven't arrived** yet.

## When to use it
### 1. Life experience (ever / never)
The time is not important – only that it happened in your life.
- I **have been** to Japan.
- **Have** you **ever eaten** sushi? – No, I **never have**. / No, I've **never eaten** it.
- She **has never seen** snow.
- This is the best film I'**ve ever seen**.
### 2. Past action with a present result
- I'**ve broken** my leg. (I can't walk now.)
- He **has lost** his wallet. (He doesn't have it now.)
- Someone **has stolen** my bike!
- Look, I'**ve bought** a new dress!
### 3. Recent actions (just, already, yet)
- I'**ve just finished** my homework. (a moment ago)
- She **has already left**. (sooner than expected)
- **Have** you **finished** **yet**? / I **haven't finished** **yet**.
### 4. Unfinished time (for / since)
The action started in the past and **continues now**.
- I **have lived** here **for** ten years. (I still live here.)
- She **has worked** at the bank **since** 2020.
- We **have known** each other **since** childhood.
- How long **have** you **been** married?
| for + a period of time | since + a point in time |
| for two hours | since 9 o'clock |
| for three days | since Monday |
| for a week | since last week |
| for ten years | since 2016 |
| for a long time | since I was a child |
### 5. Unfinished time periods (today, this week, this year)
- I'**ve drunk** three cups of coffee **today**. (today is not finished)
- She **hasn't called** me **this week**.

## Present perfect or past simple?
This is very important!
| Past simple – finished time | Present perfect – unfinished / no time |
| I **saw** the film **yesterday**. | I'**ve seen** the film. (no time said) |
| She **went** to Paris **in 2019**. | She'**s been** to Paris. (in her life) |
| I **lost** my keys last week (but I found them). | I'**ve lost** my keys. (I can't find them now.) |
| He **lived** in Delhi for 5 years (now he doesn't). | He'**s lived** in Delhi for 5 years (and still does). |
| **When did** you **arrive**? | **Have** you **arrived** yet? |
**Golden rule**: If you say **when** (yesterday, last week, in 2015, two days ago), use the **past simple**.
! I have seen him yesterday. => I **saw** him yesterday.
! I have visited Goa in 2020. => I **visited** Goa in 2020.
! When have you arrived? => When **did** you arrive?
! I live here since 2010. => I **have lived** here since 2010.
! I know him for five years. => I **have known** him for five years.
! I am here since morning. => I **have been** here since morning.

## Been or gone?
- She **has gone** to the market. (She is there now – not here.)
- She **has been** to the market. (She went and came back.)
- **Have** you ever **been** to Mumbai? (visited and returned)

# Present Perfect Continuous

## What is it?
The present perfect continuous is for an action that **started in the past** and **has continued until now** (or has just stopped). The focus is on **how long** or on the **activity** itself.
- I **have been waiting** for you for an hour!
- It **has been raining** all day.

## How to make it: have / has + been + verb-ing
| Positive | Negative | Question |
| I / You / We / They **have been** working | I **haven't been** working | **Have** you **been** working? |
| He / She / It **has been** working | She **hasn't been** working | **Has** she **been** working? |
- How long **have** you **been learning** English?
- She **has been studying** since 8 o'clock.

## When to use it
### 1. Duration up to now (how long?)
- I'**ve been working** here **for** five years.
- They'**ve been talking** on the phone **since** 7 p.m.
- How long **have** you **been living** in Pune?
### 2. Recent activity with visible results
- You look tired. **Have** you **been running**? (You are sweating.)
- Why are your hands dirty? – I'**ve been fixing** the car.
- It **has been raining**. The streets are wet.

## Present perfect simple or continuous?
| Present perfect simple | Present perfect continuous |
| Result / how many | Activity / how long |
| I'**ve written** three emails. | I'**ve been writing** emails all morning. |
| She'**s read** the book. (finished) | She'**s been reading** the book. (maybe not finished) |
| I'**ve painted** the room. (done) | I'**ve been painting** the room. (that's why I'm dirty) |
> **Tip:** With stative verbs (know, like, have = own, be), use the simple form: "I've **known** her for years." (not "I've been knowing")
EX: Complete with the present perfect continuous
1. I ___ (wait) for the bus for 20 minutes. || have been waiting
2. She ___ (learn) English since January. || has been learning
3. It ___ (rain) all morning. || has been raining
4. How long ___ you ___ (live) here? || have ... been living
5. They ___ (play) football for two hours. || have been playing
6. He's tired because he ___ (work) all day. || has been working

2. I've ___ (read) this book all week. || been reading
3. She has ___ (know) him for ten years. || known
