# At a Restaurant or Café

## Useful phrases
| Customer | Waiter / Staff |
| A table for two, please. | Do you have a reservation? |
| I've booked a table for 8 o'clock under the name Patel. | Right this way, please. |
| Could we see the menu, please? | Here's the menu. |
| What do you recommend? | Are you ready to order? |
| What's in this dish? / Is it spicy? | What would you like to drink? |
| I'd like the vegetable biryani, please. | Anything else? |
| I'll have the same. | Would you like a starter / dessert? |
| Can I have it without onions, please? | How would you like your steak? (rare / medium / well done) |
| Could we have some more water, please? | Is everything OK with your meal? |
| Excuse me, I ordered the chicken, not the fish. | I'm so sorry. I'll change it right away. |
| Could we have the bill, please? (UK) / Check, please. (US) | Would you like to pay together or separately? |
| Can I pay by card? | Here's your receipt. |
| Is service included? | Thank you. Have a nice evening! |
| Keep the change. | |

## Dialogue 1: Ordering dinner
Waiter: Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
Customer: Yes, a table for two under the name Sharma.
Waiter: Ah yes, Mr Sharma. Follow me, please. Here's your table. Here are the menus.
Customer: Thank you.
Waiter: Can I get you something to drink?
Customer: Could we have a bottle of water and two mango lassis, please?
Waiter: Of course. Are you ready to order, or do you need a few minutes?
Customer: We need a few more minutes, please.
Waiter: No problem.
(Five minutes later)
Waiter: Are you ready to order now?
Customer: Yes. What do you recommend?
Waiter: The paneer tikka is very popular. And our fish curry is excellent.
Customer: Is the fish curry very spicy?
Waiter: It's medium spicy. We can make it mild if you like.
Customer: Great. I'll have the fish curry, mild, with rice. And my wife would like the paneer tikka with two naans.
Waiter: Anything else?
Customer: No, that's all for now, thank you.
(After the meal)
Customer: Excuse me, could we have the bill, please?
Waiter: Certainly. Here you are.
Customer: Can I pay by card?
Waiter: Yes, of course.
Customer: Thank you. Everything was delicious.

## Dialogue 2: At a café
Barista: Hi! What can I get you?
Customer: Can I have a cappuccino, please?
Barista: Sure. What size – small, medium or large?
Customer: Medium, please.
Barista: Would you like anything to eat?
Customer: Yes, one of those chocolate muffins, please.
Barista: For here or to take away?
Customer: To take away, please.
Barista: That's 320 rupees, please.
Customer: Here you are.
Barista: Thanks. Your coffee will be ready in a minute.
EX: Restaurant language
1. Who says "Are you ready to order?" – the waiter or the customer? || the waiter
2. How do you ask for the price list of food? || Could we see the menu, please?
3. How do you ask to pay? || Could we have the bill, please?
4. "For here or to take away?" means... || Will you eat here or take the food with you?

# Shopping

## Dialogue 3: Buying clothes
Assistant: Hello. Can I help you?
Customer: Yes, please. I'm looking for a shirt for work.
Assistant: What size are you?
Customer: Medium, I think.
Assistant: These ones are very popular. They're 100% cotton.
Customer: Do you have this one in blue?
Assistant: Let me check... Yes, here you are.
Customer: Can I try it on?
Assistant: Of course. The changing rooms are over there.
(A few minutes later)
Customer: It's a bit tight. Do you have a bigger size?
Assistant: Here's a large.
Customer: This fits perfectly. How much is it?
Assistant: It's 1,200 rupees, but there's 20% off today, so it's 960.
Customer: Great, I'll take it.
Assistant: How would you like to pay?
Customer: By card, please.
Assistant: Could you enter your PIN? ... Thank you. Here's your receipt.
Customer: Can I return it if there's a problem?
Assistant: Yes, within 14 days with the receipt.
Customer: Perfect. Thank you!

## Dialogue 4: Returning something
Customer: Excuse me. I bought these headphones yesterday, but the left side doesn't work.
Assistant: Oh, I'm sorry about that. Do you have the receipt?
Customer: Yes, here it is.
Assistant: Would you like to exchange them or get a refund?
Customer: I'd like to exchange them for a new pair, please.
Assistant: No problem. Here you are. Let's test them first... Is that OK?
Customer: Yes, they work perfectly now. Thanks for your help.

## Dialogue 5: At the market (bargaining)
Customer: How much are the mangoes?
Seller: 200 rupees a kilo.
Customer: That's a bit expensive. Can you do 150?
Seller: 180. They're very fresh – they came this morning.
Customer: OK, 180. Can I have two kilos, please?
Seller: Sure. Anything else?
Customer: Half a kilo of tomatoes, please.
Seller: That's 400 in total.
Customer: Here's 500.
Seller: And here's your change, 100. Thank you!

# On the Phone

## Phone phrases
| Situation | Phrases |
| Answering | Hello? / Hello, this is Priya speaking. / Good morning, ABC Company. How can I help you? |
| Saying who you are | Hi, it's Ravi. / This is Ravi from XYZ. (not "I am Ravi") |
| Asking for someone | Can I speak to Mr Khan, please? / Is Anita there? / Could you put me through to the sales department? |
| Asking who is calling | Who's calling, please? / May I ask who's calling? |
| Asking to wait | Hold on a moment, please. / Just a second. / Please hold. |
| Person not available | I'm afraid he's not available right now. / She's in a meeting. / He's out of the office. |
| Taking a message | Can I take a message? / Would you like to leave a message? |
| Leaving a message | Could you ask him to call me back? / Please tell her that Ravi called. |
| Checking information | Could you spell that, please? / Could you repeat the number? / Let me read that back to you. |
| Problems | Sorry, the line is bad. / You're breaking up. / I can't hear you very well. / Could you speak up a bit? |
| Ending | Thanks for calling. / I'll call you back later. / Talk to you soon. Bye! |

## Dialogue 6: A business call
Receptionist: Good morning, Green Tech Solutions. How can I help you?
Caller: Good morning. Could I speak to Mr Fernandes, please?
Receptionist: May I ask who's calling?
Caller: This is Neha Kapoor from Star Electronics.
Receptionist: Thank you, Ms Kapoor. Please hold... I'm sorry, Mr Fernandes is in a meeting right now. Can I take a message?
Caller: Yes, please. Could you ask him to call me back this afternoon? It's about our order.
Receptionist: Of course. Could I have your number, please?
Caller: It's 98450 12367.
Receptionist: Let me repeat that: 98450 12367. Is that right?
Caller: Yes, that's right.
Receptionist: I'll give him the message.
Caller: Thank you very much. Goodbye.
Receptionist: Goodbye, and thank you for calling.

## Dialogue 7: Making an appointment
Receptionist: Good afternoon, City Dental Clinic.
Caller: Hello. I'd like to make an appointment with the dentist, please.
Receptionist: Have you been here before?
Caller: No, this is my first time.
Receptionist: OK. What's the problem?
Caller: I have a toothache. It started two days ago.
Receptionist: I see. We have an appointment tomorrow at 10:30 or on Thursday at 4. Which is better for you?
Caller: Tomorrow at 10:30, please.
Receptionist: Can I have your name, please?
Caller: Amit Verma. That's V-E-R-M-A.
Receptionist: Thank you, Mr Verma. So that's tomorrow, 10:30, with Dr Iyer. Please come 10 minutes early.
Caller: Great. Thanks a lot.
> **Tip:** Phone numbers: say each number separately. "0" can be "oh" or "zero". "44" = "double four". "999" = "nine nine nine" or "triple nine".

# At the Doctor and Pharmacy

## Dialogue 8: At the doctor's
Doctor: Good morning. Please sit down. What seems to be the problem?
Patient: Good morning, doctor. I've had a bad cough and a sore throat for four days.
Doctor: Do you have a fever?
Patient: Yes, last night it was 38.5.
Doctor: Any other symptoms? Headache? Body pain?
Patient: Yes, my whole body aches, and I feel very tired.
Doctor: Let me check your throat. Open your mouth and say "ah"... Your throat is very red. Now, take a deep breath... and again.
Patient: Is it serious?
Doctor: No, don't worry. It's a throat infection. I'll give you some antibiotics. Are you allergic to any medicine?
Patient: No, I'm not.
Doctor: Take one tablet twice a day after meals, for five days. And drink lots of warm water. Get plenty of rest.
Patient: Can I go to work?
Doctor: I'd stay at home for two or three days. If you don't feel better in three days, come back and see me.
Patient: Thank you, doctor.

## Dialogue 9: At the pharmacy
Pharmacist: Hello. How can I help?
Customer: Hi. I have this prescription from my doctor.
Pharmacist: Let me see... OK. This will take five minutes.
Customer: Also, do you have something for a headache?
Pharmacist: Yes, these painkillers are good. Take one or two every six hours. Not more than eight in one day.
Customer: Can I take them with the antibiotics?
Pharmacist: Yes, that's fine.
Customer: Thank you. How much is that altogether?
Pharmacist: 450 rupees, please.

## Dialogue 12: Asking for directions

# Job Interviews

## Common interview questions and model answers
### 1. "Tell me about yourself."
"I'm a recent commerce graduate from Delhi University. During my studies, I did a six-month internship at an accounting firm, where I learned to prepare financial reports and work with clients. I'm hardworking, organised, and I enjoy working with numbers. I'm now looking for a full-time position where I can grow my skills."
### 2. "Why do you want to work here?"
"I've read a lot about your company, and I really admire your focus on customer service. I believe my skills would be a good fit, and I'd love to learn from your experienced team."
### 3. "What are your strengths?"
"I'm a fast learner and very reliable. For example, at my last job, I learned a new software system in one week and then trained my colleagues."
### 4. "What is your biggest weakness?"
"I sometimes spend too much time trying to make everything perfect. I'm learning to manage my time better by setting clear deadlines for myself."
### 5. "Where do you see yourself in five years?"
"In five years, I hope to be in a senior role, managing a small team, and continuing to develop my skills in this field."
### 6. "Why did you leave your last job?"
"I enjoyed my last job, but I was looking for new challenges and more opportunities to grow."
### 7. "Do you have any questions for us?"
"Yes. What does a typical day look like in this role?" / "What are the next steps in the process?" / "How do you measure success in this position?"
## Interview tips
- Arrive 10–15 minutes early.
- Smile, make eye contact and shake hands firmly.
- Listen carefully to each question. If you don't understand, ask: "Could you repeat the question, please?"
- Give examples from your experience.
- Don't speak badly about your old company.
- At the end, say: "Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing from you."
- Practise your answers out loud **many times** before the interview.
EX: Interview language
1. How do you ask the interviewer to repeat a question? || Could you repeat the question, please?
2. What do you say at the end of an interview? || Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing from you.
3. Complete: "I'm a fast ___ and very reliable." || learner
4. Is it a good idea to speak badly about your old boss? || No

# More Everyday Dialogues

## Dialogue 16: Meeting a new neighbour
Sara: Hi! I'm Sara. I've just moved into the flat next door.
Kumar: Oh, hello! Welcome to the building. I'm Kumar.
Sara: Nice to meet you, Kumar. Have you lived here long?
Kumar: About five years. It's a nice, quiet area.
Sara: That's good to hear. Is there a supermarket near here?
Kumar: Yes, there's one just round the corner, next to the pharmacy. It's open until 10.
Sara: Great, thanks. And which bus goes to the city centre?
Kumar: Number 42. The bus stop is at the end of the street.
Sara: Thanks so much. You've been very helpful.
Kumar: No problem. If you need anything, just knock on my door.

## Dialogue 17: Making plans with a friend
Anil: Hey Priya! Are you free this Saturday?
Priya: I think so. Why?
Anil: There's a new comedy film at the cinema. Do you want to see it?
Priya: Sounds great! What time?
Anil: There's a show at 6:30. We could have dinner after.
Priya: Perfect. Where shall we meet?
Anil: How about at the café opposite the cinema, at 6?
Priya: OK. See you there!
Anil: Great. I'll book the tickets online.

## Dialogue 18: Complaining politely
Guest: Excuse me. I'm in room 305. I'm afraid there's a problem with my room.
Receptionist: I'm sorry to hear that. What's the problem?
Guest: The air conditioning isn't working, and it's very hot.
Receptionist: I apologise for that. I'll send someone to fix it right away.
Guest: Thank you. Also, there are no towels in the bathroom.
Receptionist: I'm very sorry. Housekeeping will bring some immediately. If the AC can't be fixed, we'll move you to another room.
Guest: That would be great. Thank you for your help.

## Dialogue 19: At the bank
Customer: Good morning. I'd like to open a savings account.
Bank clerk: Certainly. Do you have your ID and proof of address?
Customer: Yes, here's my Aadhaar card and an electricity bill.
Bank clerk: Thank you. Please fill in this form. Do you want a debit card too?
Customer: Yes, please. How long will it take to get it?
Bank clerk: About seven working days. It will be sent to your address.
Customer: Is there a minimum balance?
Bank clerk: Yes, 5,000 rupees.
Customer: OK. And can I use online banking?
Bank clerk: Yes. You'll receive the login details by email.

## Dialogue 20: Talking about the weekend
Lisa: Hi Raj! How was your weekend?
Raj: It was great, thanks. I went to my cousin's wedding in Jaipur.
Lisa: Oh, wow! How was it?
Raj: Amazing. There was lots of music and dancing, and the food was delicious. We danced until 2 a.m.!
Lisa: That sounds like so much fun. Did you take any photos?
Raj: Hundreds! I'll show you later. What about you? What did you do?
Lisa: Nothing special. I cleaned my flat and watched a few episodes of a new series.
Raj: Sometimes a relaxing weekend is the best kind!
Lisa: True!
> **Tip:** Read each dialogue **out loud** three times. First, read both parts. Then cover one part and try to say it from memory. Finally, change some details (names, places, food) and make your own dialogue.

### Example answer: Topic 25 – Learning English
"I'm learning English because I want to get a better job. In my city, many companies want employees who can speak English well. When I started, I was very nervous about speaking. I knew some grammar, but I couldn't say anything! Now I practise every day. I read this book, I watch videos on YouTube, and I talk to myself in the mirror. The most difficult thing for me is listening, because native speakers talk so fast. But I'm getting better. My goal is to have a job interview in English next year."
